# Game contract

Plays assembled city artifacts in a continuous first-person scene with physics, simulation, quests and saved progress.

## Input

- `new GameApp(config).start()` starts the browser session; `GameApp.configFromUrl()` reads the [query and parsed settings](data/schema/game-config.d.ts). The application entry is `?mode=game`. The developer readouts (position, loaded files, frame stats) show in a preview and stay hidden in a catalog game, unless `details=on|off` asks otherwise; the Settings panel turns them on and off.
- `game` loads the [saved descriptor](persistence/schema/game-state.schema.json) from `/out/games/<id>` and owns that directory. Without it, `out` is a session-only preview. The default preview is `/out/city-tiny` with Atlas sample `city-urbe-tiny`.
- [World manifest](../assembly/schema/world-manifest.schema.json): exact shell IDs, selected interiors, floor tags, source versions and optional archive, Connections, shell catalog, rooftop, plan index and per-parcel source references. The carried blueprint is preferred. `kit` names the plan index `kit.json`, its sha256 and, with `shared`, where it stands in the store under `/out/shared`; the index names each plan's shell and blueprint relative to that store. `sources` maps a parcel id to `kit`, `shell` or `empty`, a parcel marked `kit` requires that index, and an empty lot is named here and in no other list, so nothing is ever fetched for it. `buildings` names each kit parcel's block template, lot slot and the plan it is a copy of, whose blueprint is what that building's own is composed from. `streets` names `streets/manifest.json` with kit and blueprint hashes, and `sharedKit` where the street kit stands under `/out/shared`. `interiorModules` names `modules.json` the same way, and a world with interiors must publish it; the optional `interiorProps` names the furniture catalog beside it. A manifest without them is a world of generated shells. A declared Connections artifact requires matching seeds and byte hashes; manifests without it retain in-process Connections generation and sample fallback.
- [World Archive](../world-archive/CONTRACT.md) validates bounded part hashes and counts. With prepared Connections, runtime reads omit optional Atlas construction planning proofs.
- [Building sources](data/schema/world-buildings.d.ts): Exterior blueprint for every listed parcel, with a GLB for a shell parcel and a placement table plus the parcel's own sign `word` for a kit one; NPC data, the Interior building manifest and its three placement layouts only for manifest interiors. Source documents and plan blueprints share eight concurrent requests, and a plan's blueprint is read once for the city whether a parcel or the kit runtime asks for it. A world with a shell catalog loads all interiors plus sources within 250 m of the player (or the first interior); a world without a catalog loads every listed source.
- [Materials](../../../materials/CONTRACT.md) catalogs under `/materials`, character and vehicle assets under `/models`, and optional Naming NPC types beside the world. Missing NPC types use Simulation's built-in set.
- [Quest Bundle](../quest-bundle/CONTRACT.md) validates the Quests handoff. A catalog descriptor with `questBundle: null` loads no quest, investigation or scenery files. Direct previews also accept standalone questlines, investigations and scene specs. Every questline is stamped from the city at load (venue names on its places, the hour its text names on its steps) and cast against the Naming types through the Quests story venues, one person per character across the set.

## Output

- A browser play session. `tick(delta)` advances elapsed seconds through clock, physical actors, interaction and rendering. Its player step (`stepPlayer(delta)`) runs physics, the crowd's and traffic's push, the lifts carrying a rider, then the camera, so a rider's eye stays at one height in a moving car; a player the [fall guard](physics/CONTRACT.md) finds out of the world then stands again at the last point they stood on the street (or were put at with `placePlayer`), and the console names where the world let them through. Startup errors remain on the loading view; `start()` catches them.
- `placePlayer(feet, target?)` stands the feet at a point and aims the crosshair at `target`, false while a ride carries the body. `pressAction('interact' | 'secondary-interact')` presses E or R on the next tick through the keys' owners. `sayLine(text)` sends a chat line as the chat box does and settles once its reply or failure shows. `waitUntil(timeMin)` moves the world clock on, never back, as the journal's wait does, and lets the crowd, scenery, journal and objective catch up; quest progress is unchanged. `questActionResult(result)` takes a completed or refused quest result (a QuestActions, mechanic or investigation result, `{ ok, progressed, message, completed }`) to the reading card (accepted document reads and evidence inspections), the toasts, the ending summary, the journal, the objective, the inventory and the save, as every quest path does. The [automation probe](debug/CONTRACT.md) drives play through them.
- `WorldSource.load()` returns the [building-source ports](data/schema/world-buildings.d.ts), Atlas data with `blueprintUrl` (the blueprint actually read: the world's own, or the query's Atlas sample for an older world that carries none, which the loaded-files readout names), Connections data, optional quest catalogs, NPC types, the requested game descriptor and `kit` (the validated `kit.json`, the base URL its piece files are relative to, and `blueprints`, the city's one reader for plan blueprints), or null. `interiorModules` and `interiorProps` are the shared interior catalogs with the base URL their files are relative to, or null. `nativeStreets` is the [verified saved street source](ground/native/CONTRACT.md), or null when the manifest has no `streets`. A declared street source requires its exact carried blueprint and bundle hashes; missing data fails loading. `loadBuildings(ids)` reads unique manifest IDs without owning residency. `unbuilt` lists Atlas parcels absent from the manifest.

Game composes native ordinary streets with retained ground, highways and stations through `GroundScene`. Native streets draw one batch per native surface and collide as cuboids per cell. Native features reserve prop clearance independently of residency. Exact replacement owners suppress hardware the native bundle already covers; ordinary native paint is emitted once while highway paint remains in its existing builder. Retained Atlas ground, highways and stations keep triangle collision.
- [GamePersistence](persistence/CONTRACT.md) serializes [live state](persistence/schema/live-state.schema.json) to a [save payload](persistence/schema/save-current-payload.schema.json) and verifies the [acknowledged result](persistence/schema/save-result.schema.json). Leaving a catalog game waits for that save. A catalog game also saves on its own: 1.5 s after its items or contacts change or a talk ends (changes close together make one save), every 180 s of play, and at once when the page is hidden or left, that request sent `keepalive` when it is under 60 KB so it may outlive the page. A refused save says in a toast which fields failed (`live game state does not match its schema: /inventory/0/id must match pattern …`), and the console keeps every one. A lost GPU device saves the game where it stands and reloads the page to resume from it ([recoverDeviceLoss](look/CONTRACT.md)). Position is the player's feet; heading is camera yaw in radians. `npcState` carries the continuity, the quest escort and the [companion](companion/CONTRACT.md) at one world minute; a load restores the continuity, then the escort, then the companion, with no conversation open. `scenery` carries each quest scene's lifecycle, and a load stands the staged ones again from it. What people remember of talking with the player the dialogue server keeps in the game's directory as each talk completes, so closing the game without a save loses none of it; the save's `dialogueMemory` carries it too: a load hands it to the server (`PUT /api/talk/memory`), where each person keeps the newer of the two, and each save reads it back (`GET /api/talk/memory`). Until the server has taken the loaded memory, each talk and each save hands it over first: a talk that cannot fails as a talk failure, and a save that cannot, or cannot read the memory, keeps the one it holds.
- [UI](../ui/CONTRACT.md) receives world values and action callbacks. One panel owns focus. Animation, dialogue transcript and live portrait are session presentation state. The inventory shows the carried items with their quests, a quest item drawn from its mission asset's boxes, and the player's own access cards with who issued them, what they open and how the player came by them ([ScreenRecords](ScreenRecords.js)); the codex, filled as it opens, files the carried items, the people talked to (everyone a conversation opened with in this game, and everyone the saved game's `dialogueMemory` remembers, by the simulation's name and type label) and the places stood in (`discoveredLocations`, a lot named by the word on its building, else by its use and id, with its district), each with the quests it belongs to, a person with their own face (a [portrait](portraits/CONTRACT.md) of their avatar) and a lot with its own building (its plan, pictured), each asked for as its card comes into view and drawn only while the world holds still; the map reads the objective and where the player stands while it is open, and carries each grid street's lines under the name Quests `StreetNames` gives it and each district's name (the Locator's) over its centre, which it writes close in and further out.
- Keys and pause ([PauseState](player/PauseState.js)): Tab opens the journal, M the map, I the inventory, X the codex, T the contacts, ? the controls, and Escape the settings, which replace the pause bar: the game opens paused, holding the pointer plays, and Escape on the street, or the pointer lost there, opens SETTINGS, the world holding still as it does while any full panel is open: no time passes for the clock, physics, crowd, traffic, animation, companion, scenery or water, and the NPC voice stops where it is (`NpcVoice.setPaused`). The settings carry Save, which saves a catalog game and says how it went (a preview's is disabled), and Leave. Closing any panel (Escape, its close, the dock's Play) goes back to the street and asks for the pointer again; when the browser holds it back (it does after Escape), a line says to click the view. Escape in a conversation closes the conversation only. When the game lets the pointer go for a chat, panel, chooser or summary of its own and does not get it back as that closes, the world plays on and the same line shows; Escape there opens the settings.
- Conversation lines: the chat names the person and their role (their NPC type's label), never their schedule or ids, and shows the story the conversation belongs to: while a story topic is open, why the talk matters (the step's `stake`), not the goal of talking to the person already talked to; the next objective once a reply moves the story, or the current one in a recap. A story topic opens on its scene (the step's `narrative.description`), a line set apart from speech that is never voiced nor sent to free talk, then the person's opening; the one reply that completes the step is marked as moving the story on. Story lines (an opening, the story replies, recap questions and their answers) and free talk (typed lines and streamed replies) carry their kind. A person with identity shows, above the lines of a conversation that opens with them, the turns they remember of talking with the player (`TalkClient.remembered`), tagged earlier; they are neither voiced nor sent again, and memory that arrives once the conversation has changed shows nowhere. Every NPC line (greeting, story opening, story reply, recap answer, typed reply, the answer to a chat action) takes one path: it shows without its inline cues (Quests `stripCues`, applied to the text so far while a reply streams), the NPC takes one speaking turn, and the optional line observer from `new GameApp(config, { lineObserver })` hears the raw text, cues included. `said({ conversation, line, text })` comes once per whole line and once per completed sentence of a typed reply, with `line` the ChatPanel element for `setSpeaking`, or null for a companion's line said outside a conversation. `silenced()` comes once after what was said, when the player takes the turn (a typed line, a retry, a story choice, a recap question, a chat action or another topic), a typed reply fails, or the conversation changes or closes, except a close that sends the person along, whose words play on; it never repeats without a `said` between. The reply to a story choice, a recap question or a chat action is said in the same call, right after its silence. An observer may also take `upcoming({ conversation, texts })`, the replies a newly opened topic's choices or a recap's questions would bring, which comes only when no typed reply is pending. All are called synchronously; what they throw or reject with is logged and never reaches quest state, saving or the reply. A passer-by without identity has a null `conversation.instance`. Without an observer the game speaks the lines itself through its [voice](voice/CONTRACT.md), and the person keeps talking while their audio plays; `voice=off` in the query, the settings or the chat's voices toggle silence it; the toggle and the settings show the same setting, and a caller's observer leaves the toggle out.
- Typed chat streams over `/api/talk/stream`: the NPC line shows with the first text and grows until done, and a reply that sends nothing for 90 s is given up. It carries the conversation on: every line shown that no reply carried (the greeting, a story opening, a choice or recap question and its answer, a chat action and its answer) goes once, with the minute it was shown, with the next typed line to that person as `prior` (`TalkClient.said`), and the person remembers them with the exchange. It also carries what happened around the talk as `events` ([RecentEvents](talk/RecentEvents.js)): only what would stay on anyone's mind, never a list of small things: the newest car that hit someone in the last 30 minutes, when it hit the person talked to, they stood on their feet within 60 m of it (a leader who walked the player on since), or it hit hard or left somebody lying within 30 m (noted once the fall is taken, by the building whose street access is nearest, fast or not, the person talked to when it was them, whether the person hit still lies there), and the newest staged quest scene in the street within 80 m or in the building the player stands in, but not at the place a leader brought the player to, whose notes the `guide` carries; at most 2, newest first. A talk to a body with identity also carries what the person looks like in words (`look`, [Describe](agents/avatar/Describe.js) of the recipe the renderer draws them in), where they stand (`here`: world XZ, the building and floor of the room holding them, the light there in plain words by the sky's state (`daylight`, `dusk`, `night`, `early morning`) and whether they are indoors, and inside a furnished building `building` ([BuildingFacts](talk/BuildingFacts.js) of `InteriorRoutes.plan`): what each floor holds by room kind and apartment number, how many lifts and staircases join them, the kind of room the person is in, and up to 8 others inside, nearest first, by name when the person knows them, else by their post there or their kind of person, with their floor and room) what they are doing for the player now (`task`, the companion's `taskOf`: following, leading to a place, or an errand the player asked for) and who they know (`people`, Quests `peopleKnown` over the simulation's established people: coworkers, household and neighbours with their work, hours and where they are now, the established bodies in the same building or within 20 m seen `here`, the story's names for cast people, and the names in the player's line the person does not know). Any other player turn overtakes a reply still arriving. A failed or overtaken reply leaves no partial line; a failed one offers Retry, except a request the server refuses (HTTP 400), which says Retry would not help. A passer-by without identity, named "Someone passing by", brushes the player off and the chat says it has no time to chat. Model failure leaves chat usable.
- Gains and replies where the eye is: the answer to a chosen action in the chat's row is said in the subtitle with its typing effect, as a story line is, and any reply of free talk is too while the talk window is closed. Every item the inventory shows that it did not show at its last look (a card handed over or lifted, a quest pickup, or anything an item-acquired event brings in through `itemAcquired()`) is announced once as ITEM ACQUIRED with its card's thumbnail (GameView `announce`); what the save carried is held already. A person who gives their number is announced as NEW CONTACT with their own [portrait](portraits/CONTRACT.md); both are also set apart in an open conversation's transcript. What a companion does (follows, leads to a place and how far, has brought the player there, an errand, giving up) reads as a COMPANION notice with their face. Pictures are drawn while the world holds, on a call or in a talk.
- Contacts and calls ([contacts](contacts/CONTRACT.md)): the chat's action row asks a person with identity for their number ('Can I have your number?') while the player lacks it, and a typed line may get it too (talk `offers.contact`). The person decides as a chosen companion offer is decided: a talk that asks it alone, agreeing by the `give_number` tool or declining in words; nobody to answer for them, only a friendly person gives it (`givesNumber`), the rest declining in their own line by their disposition. Given, the contact book keeps them with the minute (the save's `contacts`), a toast says so and the ask leaves the row. The contacts screen (CONTACTS, P, in the dock) lists them with their own [portrait](portraits/CONTRACT.md), role, what their day has them doing and whether they would pick up, where they were met, the last thing they said, and a call button. Calling closes the panels and lets the world play on while the call screen rings them (`PhoneCalls`, in the player's own time): asleep or gone they do not answer, a hostile person lets it ring out, a person at work who is not friendly picks up to say they cannot talk; anyone else picks up and the conversation opens over the phone (`Interactor.call`), nobody's body stopped, turned or held: the chat, in its call mode (End call, which ends the call as the call screen's does, and each line of theirs said once, in the subtitle), shows their greeting and opens its talk window, quest topics stay for meeting in person, and the talk tells them it is a call (`call`) and where their body stands (`here`, from the continuity's actor, with no events from around the player). On the phone the action row asks them to come to where the player is ('Can you meet me here?', the talk's `meet` with the player's place), which the companion runs as a walk errand to the player's spot once the call is over (`acceptFromTool` kind `meet`); a typed line may also get them to go home or to work, wait or stop what they do for the player. Hanging up, the chat's leave or Escape ends the call; the call screen shows it ended a moment, then goes. Pictures are drawn while a call rings or is talked on, as while the world holds still.
- Words and actions agree: a typed line goes with the talk so far (the server's memory and the `prior` lines), and a reply that agrees in words alone to what the line asks, or a player who accepts what the person offered a moment ago, makes the offer a tool call would (Quests `inferOffers`), taken as one: following, leading, walking, a number and a card each happen once the chat closes. People know of the player's business only what a story casts them to know and what they were there to hear: each typed exchange face to face goes with its `witnesses`, nearest first and at most 8, who remember it in their own dialogue memory as overheard (one short note per talk, marked as other people's words with who spoke, kept through saves): the person following or leading the player or walking with them on a story's escort, wherever they are; indoors anybody in the same building on the player's floor (by the room each stands in, else within 2.2 m of the player's height); on the street anybody out on it within 7 m and no floor apart, never somebody behind a building's wall. Nobody overhears a call. The place a story step points to is among a person's `ways` only when that story casts them. Venues and haunts a person walks or leads to are buildings anybody can go into (`hasInterior`), and a talk names none other around them.
- Events: `GameApp.events` is an EventTarget whose CustomEvents tell what just happened, each with its `detail`: `contact-added` `{ npcId, name }`, `item-acquired` `{ itemId, kind, label, how, issuer }`, `theft` `{ npcId, lifted, noticed }`, `save-failed` `{ reason, message, details }`, and the companion's `companion-started`, `companion-arrived`, `companion-ended` and `companion-errand` with its signal.
- Homes Interior authors as scenes (its C8 poor apartment) belong to the people the simulation houses in them ([HomeDressing](city/HomeDressing.js)): each `personal-*` holder the home publishes is dressed for the household established in that dwelling (`floor:<index>/<unit>`), its eldest first: the name card and the mailbox by the door take the household's family names and the dwelling's number, the photo card in the monitor's frame the face of a relative the simulation already established, else the resident's own, and the coat hook the work coat of the resident's trade (Interior's `personal-c8-garment-<category>` by the type's category, loaded wherever a home publishes a hook). The labels and the photo are meshes of the floor lit by their room's fill; the coat is a copy in the shared draws. A home nobody living there is established yet shows its number alone and a bare hook.
- Addresses and access ([access](access/CONTRACT.md)): every dwelling and private room of the furnished buildings has an address derived at load from what the world publishes (`Kessler Block, floor 14, apartment 1407`, `office 305`, `storage room G01`), and every door into one a scope: apartment entrances lock with their dwelling's, and each private room's doorway (offices, executive offices, kitchens behind a counter, archives, stores, plant rooms, security and server rooms) stands a door the engine builds in the building's own pocket leaves with its room's; lobbies, galleries, receptions, shops, bars, cafés, corridors and open offices stay open. Aimed at a door locked to the player the prompt says what it needs (`Locked: apartment 1407 needs an access card`) beside a shut lock mark, and E leaves it shut and solid with a notice saying the same; a card for it opens it as any door, the prompt beside an open mark, and from the private side no card is needed. The chat's action row asks a person with identity for access to the doors they hold a card for and the player lacks (`Can you give me access to your apartment?`, their home first, then their work's), and a typed line may get a copy of any of them (talk `offers.give`): the person decides in their own words (Quests `give_item`), or with no model only a friendly person hands over their own home's (`givesCard`, after what they hold against the player). Given, the inventory keeps the card (`cardId(scope)`, kind `access-card`, its label, issuer and what it opens) and announces it. R on an established person carrying a card the player lacks tries to lift it, a risk ([access](access/CONTRACT.md) `pickpocket`): rarely in front of them and seen there, better from behind or while they are busy, a failed try mostly felt; one who notices says so and thinks a step less of the player from then on (the save's `access.regard`), and anyone tried is on guard for 30 game minutes. A talk with a person with identity carries `addresses`: their home and work by address, where inside a furnished building their body stands now, the cards they carry, how often they caught the player lifting one, and `ways`: how far their home and their work (by their doors, a post, else the building), the place the followed story step points to and up to two addresses the line names lie from where they stand, measured on the walk routes (else the straight line and a quarter), to 10 m under 100 m and 50 m beyond, with the compass point, the minutes at 1.4 m/s and a lift `up` or `down` to another floor. Quest items with Quests `opens` open their door too, and stepping inside the dwelling or room an active `access` step names by its `door` sends that step's `accessed` event. Cards and regard persist in the save.
- Company: the chat's action row, and the talk window's Actions tray, hold the [companion](companion/CONTRACT.md) offers for a person with identity ('Come with me', 'Show me' a place, 'You can go now'), available or not, each with its kind's icon. Choosing one shows as the player's line; an offer the companion's rules refuse, and a dismissal, are answered by the rules in the person's own line, and an available one is the person's own decision as a typed request is: a talk that asks it alone (`talkOffers([offer])`), the person agreeing by the tool or declining in words, the actions staying; when nobody can answer for the person, they decide by their disposition (`accept` with `willing`). A typed request carries the talk's wide list and the actions the person may take (walk somewhere, stop, go home or to work, wait, sit), and an agreement to any of them closes the chat on the person's words and starts it once the chat is closed. A typed request carries the available offers (the wide list) and, for a leader at its place, the place (`guide`); the first offer the whole reply makes is the player's consent and goes to `acceptFromTool`, refused ones answered in a line. Agreed either way, the chat closes on the person's words, a toast keeps them, the body is held (`Interactor.close` with `keep`) and the companion starts it on the next frame, when a toast names the place a leader is taking the player to, whatever the person said. A leader walks to the doorstep outside the place's main entrance, or for a place without a door to where its access path meets the pavement, and turns to the player there, in sight; its arrival opens the conversation by itself (`Interactor.talkTo`), the view turning to the leader, and waits while the player has a panel, a chooser, a summary or another conversation open (the companion's `busy`): the person talks about the place in a streamed, voiced reply to the arrival's unseen question, which carries no offers since the player asked nothing, or says their arrival line when the model fails; a conversation already open with them takes the place with its next turn. What the companion says on the way, a refusal after the chat closed and a leader with no body to talk to show as a toast under the person's name, heard with `line` null, never over a conversation; every companion notice (where a leader is taking the player, that it has brought them there, a give-up) shows as a Companion toast. Every frame the continuity hears the parcel and floor of the room the player stands in (`playerPlace`), so a follower comes in through the door, and the companion updates right after it. Waiting for a quest window is refused while anybody walks with the player.
- Quest scenery: the [scenery director](scenery/CONTRACT.md) stands each scene the quest bundle (or a preview's `quests/scenery.json`) specifies while its quest calls for it, and decides when each investigation scene stands, so evidence is offered only while its scene is staged. It is created from the save's `scenery` with the investigations as its overlay, and updates every frame after the crowd; a quest that moves (a dialogue choice, a completed action or investigation, an arrival, a transit step, a wait) runs its pass at once, so the save that follows holds what that move staged or retired. Its places resolve against the building sources, the main entrances, the Atlas plan and what stands solid on the street: lamp posts and their heads, the native street features except tree grates, and the placed props, a tree by its trunk and low branches. Its scenes warm through the renderer's warm-up queue before they show. A staged scene is a place a companion may lead to (relation `scene`, named as the companion names the parcel), carrying the scene's notes, so on arrival the person talks about what the scene shows; scenes in one parcel are one place with all their notes. Walkers in the crowd keep out of the bodies and fixed props standing in street scenes (`blockers()`): they turn back before them.

## Following a story

The quest log's pick is the questline the game follows: the HUD objective, the minimap mark and the walking route all come from it, and with no pick they come from the main story. Every questline's open places keep their own ring and their own cast whichever one is followed.

World location, quest place references and saved location first use the streamed room, then the actual published ground-floor building outline, then the original Atlas lot. Thus a kit building merged over a neighbouring lot keeps its host identity across its occupied footprint without changing the Atlas or claiming setbacks and courtyard holes. Once the player is inside the objective's occupied building, the outdoor entrance route clears; the HUD names the actual marked cast person and floor cue with their live distance instead of routing back to the street.

A new game opens on its story: when the loaded catalog game has no saved play time, the main questline's `prologue` shows in the story card over the city (`openingCard(persistence, quests)`). Begin or its close button hands over the mouse and play starts; Escape closes it onto the paused street a new game opens on, the line saying to click the view. A side job waiting for a main story step joins the journal with a toast when the main story reaches it. The quest log keeps it to read again. A direct `out` preview opens without it.

A conversation opens with authored story dialogue and explicit player replies. Questions and reminders leave progress unchanged; only an accepted completing choice advances its exact quest and step. Opening, closing, and optional free chat never complete a talk objective. The journal distinguishes inspecting, following a quest, following an alternative destination, and committing to an ending. Choice completion refreshes the objective/inventory and queues a save while the NPC remains in conversation. When the player leaves a talk, a person an open step still names stays held where they stand; anybody else, the story done with them, stands there a few seconds turned to the player before walking back into their day, and never walks out through a wall to the pavement to reach a post they already stand at. A saved conversation is released or held for its still-active appointment on reload because its modal is not restored. Ending replies remain readable; End conversation opens the outcome, and Continue returns control. The objective refreshes every four seconds, including conversation availability and clock gates.

## Preparation and residency

Ground renders out to the camera far plane (900 m); a prop renders within its size-based reach under that same window ([props](props/CONTRACT.md)). Both collide within 256 m. Source dimensions, transforms, material keys and placement plans remain unchanged. Original shells stream for large catalogs; selected interiors remain available to Simulation. A furnished floor is its layout's module and furniture placements at that floor's elevation, drawn from catalogs loaded once for the city, and waits for complete collision before it enters those draws.

Loading reads the world documents, the street pieces, the room catalogs (of the modules and the furniture, those the furnished buildings' floors place) and the characters at the same time. The building plans are not among them: a plan is read the first time a cell that stands on it is admitted, so the load costs what stands around the spawn and not what the city publishes. Ground, props and streamed shells then prepare through the same serial warm-up queue, which builds one program per material and vertex layout and prepares a model and finish once whatever cells stand it; a cell that brings a plan the city has not read also brings whatever program that plan's batches still need. The loading view counts those units over the whole load: LoadProgress hands it its line and the record `{ label, step, done, total, part }` (what runs now, the named step it runs under, the load's units, and a pass's own counter or null), and the game's name once the world documents are read. Loading runs on without waiting for frames, because the cells around the spawn have to stand before there is one; once the city is drawn, admitting a cell is cut into steps of a few milliseconds that hand the frame back between them. The crowd's vertex animation bake runs in the load, a frame row at a time under the same budget, and its parts count on the loading view; its draws are prepared with the rest of the scene. The load then stands the player's feet on the ground under the spawn (`PlayerBody.settle`), so a saved or authored point a little inside the pavement or above it starts standing, and runs the first frame whole, a tick under the loading view; the environment probe, whose resident texture the scene reflects from the start, bakes one face per ask of the same budget, each counted, through the frame's own render context, so it builds no graphs of its own there; and one more preparation pass pins whatever program that frame was the first to ask for, so the first frame of the loop builds nothing. Once the city plays, the probe's own graphs build through the warm-up queue, one at a time and only while the world holds (the pause a game opens on, a panel) or the player stands still, and it bakes once more in its own faces, all six at once, as every rebake after is drawn. Every step of admitting a cell that holds the thread, from the loader down to the plans and the collision cook, is named in the hitch log, and the renderer's own links, releases and uploads are named by material. Static shell, link and transit collision cooks in pieces of at most 2,048 triangles across tasks; lamp posts also yield during installation. The simulation clock advances at 1:1 while lighting uses a fixed 21:00 state. WebGPU is the default backend; WebGL2 defaults to low quality, opens no GPU timestamp queries and reads `gpu n/a` on the HUD.

Crowd and car capacities default to zero, each capped at 600 by the query parser. Actual counts depend on Simulation and available routes. Names never replace stable IDs. Movement follows published `path3` heights. Quest actions retain exact item, NPC, step and place identity; rejected actions do not advance progress. Saved NPC continuity and Simulation replay use the same world minute.

## Errors

- `E_WORLD_SHELL_CATALOG`: invalid catalog schema, hash, source identity, bounds or heights.
- `E_KIT_PIECES` and `E_KIT_PLACEMENT`: an unreadable plan shell, whose parcels stay empty lots while the city keeps playing, or a placement record naming a plan the world lacks, published by the [Kit runtime](city/kit/CONTRACT.md).
- `E_INTERIOR_MODULE`, `E_INTERIOR_PROP` and `E_INTERIOR_LAYOUT`: an unreadable shared module or furniture model, or a building naming a layout it does not carry, published by [City](city/CONTRACT.md).
- `E_WORLD_BUILDINGS`: invalid requested IDs or missing finite player position for selective loading.
- `E_WORLD_CONNECTIONS`: invalid declared Connections data or source/hash mismatch.
- `E_MOVEMENT_PATH3`: missing or invalid authoritative movement path.
- `E_HIGHWAY_STRUCTURE`: invalid source highway geometry.
- `E_DOOR_MOTION`: incomplete or invalid authored door travel.
- Ground, props, shell streaming, hydrology, physics, quests and persistence publish their own error sets in the linked interfaces. Other fetch, decode and renderer failures propagate as messages to the host.

General unresolved material keys render magenta and are counted; bound mission and water material failures can reject admission. Complete-game packaging, uniform resource admission, per-floor roles, observed-actor reconciliation, map presentation and large-world acceptance are proposals in [Issues](../../docs/ISSUES.md).
