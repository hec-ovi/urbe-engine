# Surface detail

Lays world-space use over the city's streets, sidewalks, curbs and exterior walls: oily, glossy smears and fingerprints in the roughness, slow dirt and tone variation that hides every tile repeat, grime in seams, corners, gutters and along a wall's base, slab rims scuffed and coatings chipped to the concrete, tyre tracks polished into the lanes, curb arrises chipped, rain streaks, and sparse stains, cracks and gum. It changes a material's colour and roughness only, as nodes built with the material, so it adds no draw, no program beyond the material's own and no work per frame.

## In

- `SurfaceDetail.load({ binding?, baseUrl?, mapTexture?, open?, anisotropy?, size? })`: the Materials [`surface-detail` binding](../../../../materials/bindings/surface-detail.json) or null, the public URL its `themes/` paths are served under (default `/materials`), a port that loads the atlas as a data texture (`PbrMaterialFactory.dataMap(key, variantId, map, { srgb, wrap })`, returning `{ texture, ready }`), a worker factory for the packing (where it answers null, as under Node, the stand-ins alone are packed in place), anisotropy and the packed fields' side (default 512).
- The binding's masks, each the red channel of its named map at its `worldSize`: `smudge` (broad smears), `fingerprint`, `macro` (dust and dirt), `edge` (scuffs along a slab's 0..1 border), `edgeChips` (chips along it) and `dirtStreaks` (rain streaks). Its `atlas` is read when it is four columns by four rows: `basecolor` as sRGB colour with alpha coverage, rows oil and tyre, crack, water mark, gum and paint chip, row 0 at the top of the image.

## Out

- A `SurfaceDetail` with `roles`, `resources` (`{ texture, ready }` for every texture a role reads), `ready` and `source` (per role, `procedural` or the binding's `key#variant`, settled to what was actually read once `ready` resolves).
- Two packed RGBA fields and the atlas, three texture units at most whatever is read. `masks`: smudge (red), fingerprint (green), macro dirt (blue) and edge breakup (alpha), read in world metres. `frames`: slab scuffs (red) and chips (green), read in a slab's own UV, and rain streaks (blue), read in world metres. Each channel the binding names is read from its map on a worker, resized to the field; world masks are spread evenly over their range by their own histogram, so a threshold in a profile covers the same share of a surface whichever source it came from, and slab frames keep their values. Every other channel, and the atlas where the binding has none, is the procedural stand-in drawn from a fixed seed ([DetailMasks.js](DetailMasks.js), [DetailPack.js](DetailPack.js)): smudge at 4.1 m a repeat, fingerprint at 0.31 m, dirt at 2.4 m, edge at 1.15 m and streaks at 2 m. Fields are unflipped with no UV transform, repeat (the atlas clamps) and are mipmapped. No shader changes once materials are built.
- `sample(role, q, stretch?)` reads a world mask at world metres `q`, `stretch` times its repeat (the dirt read eight times larger is the city-scale variation); `frame(role, uv)` reads a slab mask; `decal(q, { cell, presence, cells, seed })` scatters atlas stains over square cells of `cell` metres, `presence` (a number or node) the share of cells that carry one and `cells` the atlas runs `[row, first column, columns]` it draws from, each mark chosen, flipped, turned and scaled by a hash of its cell and kept inside it, read at the plane's own footprint so a cell border never picks a coarse mip. Oil stains, tyre marks, cracks, water marks, gum and paint chips are placed artifacts this way, never one repeated per slab; paint chips fall only on a coated walking band, never on bare slabs.
- Profiles ([SurfaceWear.js](SurfaceWear.js)) take a material's colour and roughness nodes, and for a street surface its placement's wear (0..1), and return both worn. They modulate the authored roughness rather than replace it: oily smears gloss it down toward a thin film and keep its own variation, scuffed rims, chips, grime and dirt dull it up only where they lie, and a placement's wear scales how much smearing, scuffing, grime and how many stains it shows (from 0.65 at no wear to 1.55 at full wear). A slab whose UV is one 0..1 square also takes its own tone and gloss, hashed from the world corner its UV square starts at (found from the screen derivatives of position, taken relative to the camera, and UV), so neighbouring slabs differ as separately poured ones do. Profiles: `road`, `sidewalk` (`panel` for a slab whose UV is one 0..1 square, so its border is the seam and its frame masks apply; `walk` for a coated walking band, polished down its middle with V across and chipped to the concrete; `stains: drain` for a drain cover or grate, oil and water marks only), `curb`, `gutter`, `joint`, `paint`, `wall` and `metal`, and `kept-wall` and `kept-metal`, which show half the streaks, grime and smudges and no stains; a poor wall cracks here and there. The highway profiles ([HighwayWear.js](HighwayWear.js)) read where the part stands in its structure, never a texture repeat or world height zero: `highway-pier` splashes and soaks the foot to knee height above that pier's own base and runs rust-tinted water down from the bearing seat, `highway-soffit` gathers soot and damp deposits along each joint's station, `highway-deck` runs soot streaks down the fascia, heaviest under the joints, and `highway-barrier` lays tyre spray and black rubber smears low on the road face, polishes the cap and runs rain down both faces. `graffiti(atlas, surface)` sprays four pier faces in five with a 2 x 1 m sheet of marker tags (the thin tag sheets one time in four) at chest height and one in three with a second above, each face its own sheet and placement. Road lanes are read off the piece's own X, a 3.5 m lane with tyre tracks 0.85 m off its centre. Walls read their wear on the plane they face and assume the flat city's ground at height 0.
- [Weathering.js](Weathering.js): `streetWear(surfaceId)` names the profile each native street surface wears (props, lights, scans, displays and decals wear none); `wearStreet(detail, surfaceId, nodes, wear?)` returns the worn `colorNode`, `roughnessNode` and, on a coated surface, `clearcoatRoughnessNode`; native street materials pass the placement wear of every effect whose geometry carries the wear field. `exteriorWear(key, entry)` answers `wall` or `metal` (`kept-wall` or `kept-metal` for a `rich` or `high_rich` key) or null for a shell surface: glass, lit or emissive, blended or masked, decal, room, planting, print and cloth kinds wear none. `wearExterior(detail, profile)` returns one shared `{ colorNode, roughnessNode }` per profile, read from the material's own colour and roughness, so every surface of a profile builds the same program.
- Game wiring: the game reads the binding beside the theme, loads the layer and hands it to the PBR factory as `factory.weathering = { detail, resources, nodes }`. Native street materials take it as `{ detail }` and list its resources with their own; `shellMaterial` asks the factory for a tuned copy with `weather` for every worn exterior shell surface, which carries the profile's nodes and lists the layer's textures under `Symbol.for('urbe.material-resources')`, so warm-up uploads them before it compiles. Room modules ask for none. `off=detail` leaves the layer out of a run.

## Errors

A layer missing a role's texture throws on construction. A mask the worker cannot read is reported once and stays procedural; an atlas that fails to load leaves its texture empty, and stains read as none. A worker that fails is reported once and the stand-ins are packed on the main thread.
