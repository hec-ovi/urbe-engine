# CONTRACT: NPC agents

Purpose: materializes persistent simulation NPC identities and controls one companion (follow or lead), passenger carry, conversation, explicit crouch, quest holds and deterministic schedule return over Connections paths.

Status: the public continuity and follow API is wired into the live GameApp, Crowd, Interactor, the companion and explicit quest control adapter. GameApp gives the continuity `InteriorRoutes` over Interior's browser navigation entry (`interior/dist/nav.js`) and, each frame, the room the player stands in as `playerPlace`.

## Inputs

- Street bodies: the crowd, the traffic and the fallen rig share one `street` registry of the bodies on the road this frame. Each takes its own for a test.
- Crowd and traffic population options: [schema/population-window.schema.json](schema/population-window.schema.json). Capacity bounds resident bodies. `spawnRadius` defaults to 90 m for Crowd and 110 m for Traffic; offscreen removal adds 25 m and 30 m respectively. Crowd consumes real simulation handles within the chosen circle; Traffic rotates through Connections lanes and admits actual hidden positions with room for a car, clear of people and crossing bands. A larger capacity does not invent population or road space. `CarModels.load(capacity)` reads, builds and stands no car model for a capacity of 0. Crowd's optional `blockers` is a function the crowd calls each update for solid footprints on the pavement, `[{ center: { x, z }, width, depth, yawRadians }]`: a walker whose next step would bring its 0.34 m radius into one turns back the way it came, and one already inside walks out. Crowd's optional `interiorRoutes` (`InteriorRoutes`, GameApp shares continuity's) leads its guests to and from their seats and out through the door, and its places carry the door's `outside` step besides `inside`. Crowd's optional `surface` is the `WalkSurface` over Atlas `volumetric.ground` that outdoor feet stand on; `height(x, z)` is the top of the highest carriageway, gutter, kerb, pavement, block or open cover under a point, or null, and `paving` the highest published sidewalk top, else Ground's `SIDEWALK_HEIGHT`.
- Movement network: [schema/movement-network.schema.json](schema/movement-network.schema.json). `WalkRoutes` indexes `connections.networks.walk`; every movement edge must carry authoritative `path3`. `project` searches a 16 m grid of edge segments and returns the nearest point, ties going to the lower edge id and then its earlier segment; `route` joins both projections through a heap Dijkstra settled in (distance, node id) order. `pavementEnd(ref)` is the far end of the access edge from the `entry` node whose `ref` is a place id, or null. Scheduled transit materialization also consumes the matching Connections route's ordered stops, timetable, service window and 3D shape.
- Place anchors: [schema/places.schema.json](schema/places.schema.json). Optional loaded parcel and public transport stop positions plus interior anchor ids, kinds, floors, positions and headings (GameApp gives every floor's anchors, `buildingAnchors(npc, interior)`: each at its floor's elevation, a seat at its cushion), and a parcel's optional `doorstep`, where a leader shows it: outside its main entrance, or for a parcel without a door where its access path meets the pavement (its access point lies on the lot line, which the building may fill). Rail station ids use the simulation's `stop` place kind at the published platform level.
- Appearance request: [schema/appearance-request.schema.json](schema/appearance-request.schema.json). One already-instanced npcId and current simulation time.
- Unload request: [schema/unload-request.schema.json](schema/unload-request.schema.json). The materialized npcId whose body leaves the visible set.
- Follow start: [schema/follow-start.schema.json](schema/follow-start.schema.json). One already-instanced, live `npcId`, simulation time, player position, optional `playerPlace` and optional `pace`.
- Lead start: [schema/lead-start.schema.json](schema/lead-start.schema.json). One live npcId, simulation time, exact authored destination place, optional `pace` and optional `target` ([values.schema.json#/$defs/target](schema/values.schema.json)): a world point inside the place (`position`, `parcelId`, `floor`), such as an apartment door's corridor side, a work spot or a lift landing, or a person (`npcId`).
- Errand start and stop: [schema/errand-start.schema.json](schema/errand-start.schema.json), one live npcId, simulation time, a `target` point (inside `parcelId` when given, with optional `floor`, `heading` and `seated`) and `untilMin`; [schema/errand-stop.schema.json](schema/errand-stop.schema.json), the npcId, simulation time and optional `linger`.
- Pace: [values.schema.json#/$defs/pace](schema/values.schema.json). `giveUpBeyond` metres and `giveUpAfterMin` simulation minutes, optional `runs` (true: runs to keep up with a running player; false: never above a walk) and `hurry` (a leader jogs at 2 m/s while the player is close). A lead without one gives up beyond 60 m after 3 minutes; a follow without one never gives up.
- Ways: optional `ways` in the constructor, `{ pass(npcId, gate), ride(npcId, gate, stage) }`, the doors and lifts walkers pass. `pass` asks a door gate's door open and answers whether the body may go through; `ride` runs a lift gate: `call` answers `wait` or `board`, `ride` the car's `{ y }` or `alight`, `done` lets it go; null from either means no such door or lift is loaded, and the walker walks the way as drawn. [Passage](Passage.js) provides them over the game's door leaves (street doors and apartment doors, `wanted` set as the player's E sets it and `asked` marked on each ask, so the door, which closes by itself 5 s after the last ask, stays open while walkers keep asking) and its lift shafts (`Elevators`: the car called to the landing, pressed to the walker's floor from inside, ridden at its height). Passage's `open(gate)` opens a door as a host opens their own door to a guest; like any door it closes by itself once nobody asks or stands in it.
- Follower carry: [schema/follower-carry.schema.json](schema/follower-carry.schema.json). The active follower, measured transit position and authoritative route id.
- Follow update: [schema/follow-update.schema.json](schema/follow-update.schema.json). Current simulation time, bounded frame delta, player position and optional `playerPlace`: the parcel and floor of the building the player stands in.
- Interior routes: optional `interiorRoutes` in the constructor, `{ covers(parcelId), route(parcelId, from, to) }`, where `route` answers [schema/interior-route.schema.json](schema/interior-route.schema.json): a world `path3` inside that building from one point to the other, or null. `InteriorRoutes(buildings, { findPath })` provides it from each building's Interior navigation (`npc.nav`) and published floor elevations, `findPath` being Interior's browser navigation entry; a point stands on the highest floor at most 0.5 m above its feet. A stair connector is walked up or down the stair's own flights (the `stair-flight-N` placements each floor's layout publishes for it, tread by tread at the tread line, stepping on and off 0.55 m from foot and top), straight between its entries only where no flight is published; a lift connector is walked from its landing into the middle of its shaft room, up or down the shaft and out onto the landing of its floor, and is a `lift` gate on the route (`from`, `board`, `alight`, `to`, measured along it); a numbered apartment door the walk passes through (Interior's `apartmentEntrances`, its `position`, `inward` and `width`) is a `door` gate. `plan(parcelId)` is the building's circulation: per floor its elevation, lift landings, stair entries and rooms by kind (and unit) with a point inside each, and the apartment doors with the corridor point 0.9 m in front of each; null while the building is not loaded. Everything is read from the building's published data, so a new layout routes the same way. `buildings` is the live parcel map a streamed world fills and empties as cells load and drop: a building covers and routes only while its record is there, and what is read from a record is kept for that record alone.
- Follow stop: [schema/follow-stop.schema.json](schema/follow-stop.schema.json). Current simulation time.
- Crouch start: [schema/crouch-start.schema.json](schema/crouch-start.schema.json). One exact npcId and current simulation time. It never derives from player input or movement.
- Crouch stop: [schema/crouch-stop.schema.json](schema/crouch-stop.schema.json). The same exact npcId and current simulation time.
- Conversation start: [schema/conversation-start.schema.json](schema/conversation-start.schema.json). Exact npcId, current body position, place, heading and seated state.
- Conversation stop: [schema/conversation-stop.schema.json](schema/conversation-stop.schema.json). Current simulation time, and optional `hold`.
- Hold start: [schema/hold-start.schema.json](schema/hold-start.schema.json). One exact npcId, current simulation time, the place it is wanted at, the world point, heading and optional seated posture.
- Hold release: [schema/hold-release.schema.json](schema/hold-release.schema.json). The held npcId and current simulation time.
- Visible update: [schema/visible-update.schema.json](schema/visible-update.schema.json). Current time, player position and the offscreen virtualization distance.
- Character recipes: the NPC Studio's `codex-npcs-persona` version 1 JSON ([avatar/Recipe.js](avatar/Recipe.js)): body frame (regular, teen or athletic male; the Superhero female export is only an upper body), eleven body and six face shape controls with height 94 to 106 % of the frame, hairstyle, skin, hair and eye colours, and a top, trousers and footwear from 10, 8 and 6 fitted garments, each with primary, secondary and accent colours, over one fabric (technical, woven, leather). Validation clamps shapes to their limits, reads a later control as neutral and refuses anything the pack cannot wear. A person of the street (`personRecipe`) keeps the studio's draw for their seed of hairstyle, colouring, face, height and garments, and their clothes are coloured from the street's wardrobe on a stream of their seed's own (`streetColors`): nineteen top colours from the city's dark blues through slate, olive, rust, teal and burgundy to stone and off-white, never one near their skin, a second colour that shows against it, trousers apart from the top in denim, black, charcoal, grey, khaki, olive, brown or stone, and black, brown, tan, grey or white shoes.
- Authored characters: [avatar/templates/index.js](avatar/templates/index.js) lists template files, each a studio export as it stands (named after its file) or `{ name, recipe | outfit, crowd?, people? }`: a whole person or an outfit, the share of the street that wears its outfit (all templates together at most half), and the simulation npcIds that are this person. The shipped templates are the studio's own: District liaison (a person, 4 % of the street wears her outfit), Metropolitan patrol (4 %) and Night runner (3 %).
- Restore state: [schema/continuity-save.schema.json](schema/continuity-save.schema.json). A prior output of `serialize()` for the same restored simulation. Version 1 saves held one `follow` slot for the companion or a walk home; restore turns a `resuming` slot into a `returns` entry and gives a leader phase `walking` and the default pace.

The simulation dependency supplies `getNPC`, `continuityAt`, `interrupt` and `resume` per [the simulation contract](../../../../simulation/CONTRACT.md). Its exact continuity output is [npc-continuity.schema.json](../../../../simulation/src/schemas/npc-continuity.schema.json).

## Outputs

- Actor state: [schema/actor-state.schema.json](schema/actor-state.schema.json). Exact npcId, name, type, gender, appearance seed, scheduled place and progress, world position, heading, animation, visibility and control mode.
- Optional actor state: [schema/actor-state-or-null.schema.json](schema/actor-state-or-null.schema.json). Follow updates without a companion, unloads of unknown materializations and `actor(npcId)` for an unknown identity return null.
- Actor states: [schema/actor-states.schema.json](schema/actor-states.schema.json). Stable npcId-sorted projections for every retained materialization, including invisible virtualized actors.
- Serializable state: [schema/continuity-save.schema.json](schema/continuity-save.schema.json), version 2. Every materialized identity, the companion (`follow`: mode, phase, cached route, lead destination, pace, `lostSinceMin`), every walk home (`returns`, with `lingerUntilMin` while it has not set off), conversation, explicit crouch, quest holds and posts. A cached route keeps the building its goal stands in (`parcelId`), the stretches it walks inside buildings (`indoor`), its door and lift gates (`gates`) and a lift ride under way (`ride`: the gate and the stage, `call`, `board`, `ride` or `alight`, and `held` while a leader holds the car for the player). The leader keeps its `target` and `pending`; every errand (`errands`) its route, target and `untilMin`.
- Control events: [schema/control-events.schema.json](schema/control-events.schema.json). `drainEvents()` returns and clears the companion phase changes since the latest `updateFollow` began: `{npcId, mode, phase, timeMin}`, with `reason` (`player-lost`, `unreachable`, `unavailable`) on `gave-up`. The next `updateFollow` drops undrained events. Events are not saved.
- `Appearance.appearance({gender, appearanceSeed, npcId})`: a person's look, decided by who they are: their recipe (`look.recipe`), the authored person a template names by npcId when its body is of their gender, else the studio's generator on their seed with the templates' outfits in their shares; and what the crowd paints of it: skin, hair and eye colours, the top's two colours, the trousers' and footwear's, where the sleeve, the top's hem and collar, the trousers' hem and waist and the footwear's top end (as the garment patterns cut them, shares of body height), whether the top is tucked, how its second colour shows (plain, open front, yoke), height and hairstyle. The type plays no part.
- `describeLook(recipe)` ([avatar/Describe.js](avatar/Describe.js)): a recipe in the words the dialog layer tells a person about their looks (Quests `DialogLook`): height (short under 0.975 of the frame, tall over 1.025), build and shoulders, hips and legs worth a word, the two face features furthest from neutral, the hairstyle with its colour (a shaved head for none), the skin, hair and eye palettes by name, each garment top to toe with its main colour and where its second colour shows (an open front, the shoulders, the shoulders and sleeves, as the crowd paints it), and the fabric; `colorName(hex)` names any other colour by family and lightness.
- `CharacterPoser` (`HeroCharacter.poser`, shared with the scenery renderer): `body(id)` and `hair(path)` read a body and a hairstyle once for the run, `model(recipe)` is a recipe's body, hairstyle and fit (held until its root is released, or `drop`), `dress(source, person, name)` stands the person in their recipe (`root.userData.dressed`: their `presence`, `colors` and garment `panels`, which `wear(root, recipe)` rewrites and `wears(mesh)` names per mesh), `height(root)` and `fit(root)` are what a dressed root stands on and wears, `release(root)` undresses it and hands its fit back, `sampleWardrobe(root)` puts every garment of the catalog on a root for a warm-up, `still({gender, appearanceSeed, npcId}, clipName, at)` is the person that identity gives, held at `at` (0 to 1) of the transferred clip, 1 being its final frame, at their height, standing at the origin facing +Z, and `pose(recipe, clipName, at)` the same for a recipe (the [portraits](../portraits/CONTRACT.md) pose people with it).
- `Crowd.personLook(npc, npcId?)`: the look the crowd dresses an established person in, from the simulation's person (`getNPC`): their gender and seed, or the quest seed a cast member without one takes; its `recipe` is what a rig of them wears.
- `Tailor` ([avatar/Tailor.js](avatar/Tailor.js)): `prepare(model)` refines a body's seat surface and measures its shape controls once; `fit(model, recipe)` is the recipe's fitted body, face and garment shells, built in steps under the frame budget it is given and cached by body, surface shape and garments (`fitKey`: never colour, height or hair), counted out until `release`; past its capacity (8) the least recently used fits nobody holds are disposed.
- `HeroCharacter.near(people)`: the crowd members the host wants close (GameApp: those within 6 m of the player's eye, the companion within 15 m and first, each kept 1.5 m further out once shown, fully present and not a stress copy, leaving, frozen or down), nearest first. Up to `CLOSE_RIGS` (2) of them, the one talked to counting among them, wear a rig of their recipe built while their crowd body shows them; each follows its body's place, heading, presence, clip and baked frame (the crowd's frame is its clock, a new clip blends in over 0.25 s), so neither swap shows a pose change, and one no longer wanted gives its rig back. `rigOf(person)` is the focused or close rig showing a person, or null.
- `HeroCharacter.speak(npcId, speech)`: the person's voice plays (`speech`, `{ seed, loudness() }`, from the [voice](../voice/CONTRACT.md)) or has ended (null, which clears only that person's). While it plays, the focused rig of that person moves its head and neck to it (`SpeechGesture`).
- `HeroCharacter.lookAt(person, point)`: the person the player talks to (GameApp: the conversation's, `point` the camera) looks at `point`, and nobody does once `person` is null. Their focused rig turns its upper spine, neck and head toward it over the clip's pose (`LookAt`): the spine at most 25 degrees, the neck and head together at most 70 (four tenths the neck), up to 25 degrees up and 35 down, eased as a critically damped spring over 0.3 s and faded in and out over 0.3 s; somebody more than 125 degrees round, at their back, is not looked at. A seated person looks round from their chair; a standing one, already turned to the player, follows their eye. The look eases back to the clip on a rig the talk leaves close.
- `companion`: `{npcId, mode, phase, position}` or null, read without schema validation so a host may read it every frame. Phase is `walking`, `waiting` or `arrived`.
- `heldNpcIds` lists the identities held now. `actor(npcId)` is the retained state of one identity.

## Events

- `appear(request)` projects the NPC's actual simulation schedule. Passenger transit legs map schedule progress through the matching per-leg timetable onto the route's authoritative 3D shape. `unload(request)` removes visibility while retaining identity state. A later `appear` uses the same npcId and body traits.
- There is at most one companion, following or leading. Any number of identities walk home at once, each on its own route, and none of them blocks a new companion, crouch or hold.
- `startFollow(request)` and `startLead(request)` accept only a live, positioned NPC and interrupt its routine. They start from the body where it stands when it is on screen, held or walking home, and from its schedule projection otherwise. A held body keeps its interruption; a walk home is taken over. The companion itself may be asked: it changes mode, or destination, where it stands and keeps its interruption.
- `updateFollow(request)` advances every walk home in npcId order, then the companion, and returns the companion's state or null. Routes are cached: a follow plans again once the player is 1 m from the planned end, a walk home once its scheduled point has moved 2 m, a lead only when its route runs out short of the destination. Movement per update never exceeds speed times elapsed time.
- A follower walks at 1.4 m/s, runs at 2.4 m/s beyond 8 m (not with a pace that does not run) and with a pace that runs whenever the player runs faster than 2 m/s, and stops 1.8 m from the player; its phase is `walking` while it moves and `waiting` while it stands, facing the player. When the player stands in a building, interior routes lead the follower in through its door; without them, or with no way to the player inside, it follows a player on the ground floor within 12 m of the door and otherwise walks to the door and waits there, asking the interior routes again once the player has moved 2 m.
- Every walk (follow, lead, errand, walk home) leaves and enters a building through its main door: from inside to just inside the door, over the doorstep to the pavement, and in the same way, the crossing a door gate. With interior routes a covered building is walked inside on its navigation: along corridors, up and down its stairs, through its lifts and apartment doors, each a gate on the route; a building they do not cover is crossed straight from its door, out of the player's sight, and a walk within one building they cover but find no way through has no way. A body is in that parcel while it walks those stretches. At a door gate the walker asks the door open from 2.5 m and stands 0.6 m short of it until it is; at a lift's landing it calls the car and stands until it opens (3 game minutes at most), walks into the car's middle, rides at the car's height, and walks out where the car opens (or where the shaft lets it out once 3 minutes have passed); it walks in and out at a walk whatever its pace, and a route under way in a lift is never planned again. A leader keeps each door it went through asked open while the player is within 4 m of it, so it never shuts on them. A leader rides with the player: it boards only once the player stands within 4 m of the landing (its patience counted from then), and in the car holds it for the player to step aboard (within 1.4 m of the car's middle and a storey's rise) for half a game minute at most before it presses its floor. With no `ways`, or a gate whose door or lift is not loaded, the route is walked as drawn.
- A leader walks at 1.4 m/s while the player is within 4 m or ahead on its path (running beyond 8 m unless its pace does not run; jogging at 2 m/s close with a pace in a hurry, running with a pace that runs while the player runs), slows toward 0.8 m/s as the player lags to 10 m, and past that stops in phase `waiting`, facing the player, until the player is back within 6 m. It walks to the place's `doorstep`, else to the place position; with a `target` inside the place it walks in to it through the door, stairs and lifts, or, while the building cannot be walked yet, to the doorstep and on in once it can (`pending`); with a person `target` it walks to wherever that person stands, following them as they move, and stops 1.6 m short of them. There it takes phase `arrived` once and keeps it: it stands on the street at the place facing the player, stays where a conversation moves it, and stays leading until released.
- A companion with a pace gives up once the player has stayed beyond `giveUpBeyond` for `giveUpAfterMin` (`player-lost`). A companion also gives up when it dies (`unavailable`) or has no route to the player or destination (`unreachable`). On any give-up the simulation resumes, a `gave-up` event says why, and the body walks home when it has a route there; otherwise it is `released` where it stands.
- `carryFollower(request)` places only the active follower on the measured transit route position.
- `stopFollow(request)` resumes the simulation and the companion walks from where it stands to its current scheduled place or next destination in mode `resuming`, then returns to `schedule`.
- `stopFollow`, `endConversation` and `releaseHold` take an optional `linger`, in seconds (the hosts pass `LINGER_SECONDS`, 5): the simulation resumes at once, but the walk home first stands where the body was let go for that long, in mode `resuming` with the `idle` animation (a seated body keeps `sit` and its heading), turned to the player's position each `updateFollow`. The walk home keeps the minute it sets off (`lingerUntilMin`) through a save; a new control, conversation or hold takes the body over where it stands, and a body lingering where its day has it settles there afterwards.
- `startCrouch(request)` interrupts one actual NPC routine and holds that identity in `posing` mode with crouch animation. `releaseCrouch(request)` resumes the simulation and routes the same identity back to its current schedule.
- `beginConversation(request)` preserves the body at the visible position and pauses its routine. `endConversation(request)` walks a dialogue-interrupted NPC back into the current schedule; with `hold` it stays exactly where it stands. The companion stays interrupted and returns to following or leading when dialogue closes. Talking to anybody else never changes the companion.
- `hold(request)` interrupts one identity and keeps it at the given point in `posing` mode: the schedule pass never reprojects a held body, so the person a quest step sends the player to is still there when they arrive and after they talk. With `seated: true` it preserves a seated pose; omission stands the body. A new appointment can reclaim that identity during its walk home, while the companion, conversation and explicit pose controls retain ownership. `releaseHold(request)` resumes the simulation and walks the same identity back into its day. A follow, lead or crouch takes over a held body from where it stands.
- A body let go where its day has it already, a worker at their post or a cast member posted at their own counter, is back in `schedule` there and walks nowhere; the street graph is never asked for a way to the point a body stands on, which would lead out through the wall of the building it is in and back.
- `updateVisible(request)` reprojects visible schedule-controlled actors each frame and marks distant ones invisible without discarding identity or schedule state. A body in sight whose schedule moves it more than 1.5 m at once (from one anchor to another, in or out of a building, up to a bed on another floor) walks there as a walk home of its own, through doors, stairs and lifts, instead of jumping; a scheduled body at an anchor carries that anchor's `floor` in its place, and one walking between anchors is placed at the anchor it walks to.
- `sendOnErrand(request)` interrupts one identity (a held body, or the companion, keeps its interruption and is that no more) and walks it from where its body is to the target, where it stays in mode `errand`, seated or standing facing its heading or the player, until `untilMin`, when the simulation resumes and it walks back into its day. A talk pauses it where it stands and gives it back to the errand when it closes; a follow, lead, hold or crouch takes it over. `endErrand(request)` ends it early. `errandsUnderway` lists `{ npcId, untilMin, arrived }`. A walk home stays visible within `maxDistance`; beyond it the walk ends and the schedule projection takes the body back, invisible for that update even when the schedule place is near, and visible by distance from the next.
- `serialize()` and `restore(save)` preserve materialized body traits, world position, schedule progress and active interruption, the companion, walks home, errands, explicit pose, quest holds and posts.
- `Crowd.questMember` adopts an anonymous simulation handle when it resolves to the requested cast npcId, including with continuity enabled. `Crowd.syncActor` returns null while a body is fallen, so control and passenger projection fail closed.
- `Crowd.castMember(npcId, timeMin, player, parcelId)` is the body of one cast NPC the story wants at a parcel now, whatever its routine says: the body that npcId already owns while it is at that parcel, else an anonymous body there that resolves to it, else that identity posted within 45 m of the player at the interior's first free counter anchor, then work anchor, then a lobby spot just inside the door. With continuity the posting is a `hold`, so the next schedule pass leaves it standing instead of walking it home. Null beyond that reach, when the crowd is full, or while the owned body is fallen.
- Adopting an existing cast body also acquires its continuity hold; merely finding the right identity does not let its next schedule update take it away. A stationary body retains its actual anchor reservation through continuity updates, so separate cast members never receive the same counter/work anchor. Hosts without continuity freeze the exact retained body instead of duplicating it.
- `Crowd.castMember(..., { meeting: true })` places listening partners together in the published entrance circulation space, 1.3 m apart and facing each other. This keeps a multi-person appointment in listening reach even when a floor's counter and work anchors are far apart. An existing conversation retains control until it closes.
- `Crowd.questMember` and `castMember` name only the anonymous body holding the handle the crowd sample reports as the requested npcId. Reading the sample establishes nobody, so every other body keeps its own identity, its own look and its right to be evicted.
- `Crowd.handleFor(member, timeMin)` offers a body whose handle stopped answering another reported handle of the body's own gender that is not established and that no other body holds, or null.

## Errors

- `E_NPC_INPUT`: an API request or restore state does not match its schema.
- `E_NPC_OUTPUT`: an actor or save result does not match its schema.
- `E_NPC_UNKNOWN`: the simulation does not hold that instanced npcId.
- `E_NPC_UNAVAILABLE`: the NPC is dead or unavailable.
- `E_NPC_PLACE`: the NPC's current scheduled state has no position, including a route without a matching passenger leg or complete Connections path3 and timing facts.
- `E_NPC_PATH`: the player or scheduled destination is unreachable.
- `E_NPC_CONFLICT`: another NPC is the companion, in conversation or posed, or a release does not match the controlled identity.

## Dependencies

- Simulation 0.12 public NPC, crowd and continuity APIs: crowd samples name established people with their own seed, a crowd instantiate takes the seed a body is drawn with, and an established person lives in a real dwelling of an opened building (`home.apartment`).
- Connections walk graph and transit route output.
- [Ground](../ground/CONTRACT.md) for the retained raised-pavement datum, and Atlas `volumetric.ground` covers for the surface people stand on.
- Character asset catalog for the audited animation clips.
- Optionally, [Interior](../../../../interior/CONTRACT.md) navigation through `InteriorRoutes` for walks inside buildings.

## Presence

`SpawnVisibility(camera, {fog, occluded}).update()` refreshes the expanded frustum. `hidden(point, radius)` checks the complete body against that frustum, live fragment fog and static occlusion. Crowd and Traffic receive it as `visibility`; Crowd also receives `floorShown(parcelId, floor)`.

Street samples stay deterministic. A visible unmatched pedestrian searches up to 40 m upstream on the walk graph for a hidden entry, otherwise waits for a later refresh. Rendered population may temporarily trail the sample or retain retiring bodies, always within capacity. Cars enter at least 25 m away and rotate lane admission. Removal requires concealment beyond the normal radius plus margin; bodies still visible at the hard bounds fade. Retiring walkers use the same hysteresis as other walkers. Fallen dead people remain down until hidden. Occupants require a valid room anchor or bounded lobby position, and a body the schedule has inside a building (modes `schedule`, `resuming`, `released`) a floor it shows; a body under control (following, leading, in conversation, held) stands where the control put it.

Presence rises over 0.8 s and falls over 0.6 s. Crowd pose packs frame, clip, presence and the look's figure lane into one vec4 in the existing vertex buffer. Vehicle parts share one presence attribute. Focused, close, fallen and still rigs read their person's coverage per draw, one value for body, eyes, hair and garments. Opaque screen-space dither masks cover colour, depth and shadows on WebGPU and WebGL2. Capacity eviction prefers hidden anonymous bodies and defers while a visible victim fades. Named actors receive fractional clock minutes; visible schedule corrections converge at walking speed.

## Invariants

- Named, focused and quest NPCs are keyed only by their actual npcId. A later statistical crowd handle cannot rename one or take its body.
- Inside a building a person on duty stands at the interior's counter anchors (`counter_spot`) first, then its work spots, a guest sits on its seats, and the overflow stands in the lobby; a spot a cast body holds is never handed to the rota.
- A seated guest (`Visits.js`) is only ever the simulation guest they came in as. They sit a while (60 to 240 s, their seed's, again each time round), get up with the library's stand-up clip, walk to stand in front of a counter nobody waits at (1.35 m before its server, facing them) or else to a free lobby spot, stand there 15 to 45 s in their idle variety, and sit down with the sit-down clip on their own seat or, if somebody took it meanwhile, the first free one. When the simulation stops reporting their visit they get up where they are and walk out: to just inside the door, then through it, fading from 1.1 m before the step outside, the seat and their handle given up. A new guest whose seat the player could see walks in from the door and sits down; one out of sight is there already. Walks inside follow the crowd's optional `interiorRoutes` where they cover the building, else a straight line, at 1.05 m/s. A guest the player talks to, or the story or schedule takes over, leaves the visit where they are.
- Interior headings are +Y yaw in degrees, zero toward +Z. Seat rendering resolves the exact furniture placement instead of the navigation approach beside it, compensates the purchased sitting clip's 0.34 m rearward pelvis offset and fits the shared chair, sofa, stool and bench support planes at their published scales. Seated conversations retain the furniture heading when the player moves around the chair.
- One npcId owns one rendered body. Resolving a cast worker already present at a parcel post converts that body to continuity control without adding another body.
- A measured physics impact freezes the exact rendered identity and removes it from interaction and pushback. A rejected impact restores its prior control state. Accepted dynamic body assembly belongs to the game physics contract.
- Appearance comes from the instance's persistent `appearanceSeed`, and for a person a template names from their npcId, including after unload, save restore and reappearance. A body wears the crowd mesh its gender picks (a recipe of another frame is drawn on the regular body of its gender), and a body of unknown gender carries the gender of its mesh.
- A body keeps its mesh, skin, clothes, cuts, hair and eyebrows while anybody looks at it; its look is built again only when its seed, its mesh or the template its identity names changes. A body handed a later handle keeps the look it walks in; naming it offers that seed to the simulation, which establishes the person in it. An established person's sample is drawn in that person's own look, and never re-dresses an anonymous body in another one.
- The crowd's baked geometry, skin weights, UVs and authored normals remain intact; the crowd paints clothes on it. Baked normals interpolate across each triangle on WebGPU and WebGL. The bake (`VatBaker`) poses the skeleton for each row of every clip `CharacterCatalog.CROWD_CLIPS` lists, 12 to 32 rows by how much the clip moves (a loop's rows stop a step short of its end, a clip played once reaches its final pose), and skins every vertex on plain arrays exactly as three's own linear-blend skinning does (`VatSkin.js`), a row per ask of the load's budget; a body is a fraction of a second of main thread. A shape-keyed mesh is skinned through three's own per-vertex read instead. A focused, close, fallen or still body is its recipe's: the source body's waist, seat and upper thigh are refined once per body (every source vertex, weight and UV kept, about a fifth more triangles), a person's shape moves rest positions only (normals recomputed where they moved, authored ones kept where they did not and across UV seams), height lengthens the legs and torso through the skin matrices with bone scales left at 1 and the lowest ankle where the clip put it, and what a garment covers is hidden from the body's index.
- Pro clips transfer rotations and scaled pelvis motion onto the body's original bone lengths. Crowd, focused dialogue, impact and scenery still poses use the same transferred clips. The crowd paints each person's recipe on its baked body: skin in the recipe's colour over the pack's map (the map's median made the studio's level of the colour; the focused skin paints the same), the trousers, the top over or under their waist as it is worn with its second colour in an open front, a yoke, or a yoke and upper sleeves, and the footwear in its own colour, each ending where its pattern cuts it on the rest body and with a seam shadow at the cut, and stands at the recipe's height, the baked body scaled about its feet. Each crowd body's hair draw holds every adult hairstyle and the facial hair of its gender with its eyebrows, merged and carried by the Head bone's baked transform (three vec4 rows a frame, not a row per vertex); a person's hairstyle stands and the others fold to a point, a teen style is drawn as its adult one and none leaves the head bare, all in the recipe's hair colour over the pack's two hair maps. The focused, close, fallen and still rigs are the recipe itself: its frame, shape, face, height and hairstyle, the skin, eyes and hair in its colours, and a skinned shell per garment on the body's own skeleton with the studio's panels, trims and fabric finish (technical roughness 0.66, woven 0.9, leather 0.4), skin roughness 0.86, hair 0.96, eyes 0.3. A resident focused rig wears a new recipe's colours at once, and a recipe that builds another body on a rig built and swapped in, playing what the old one played. A body is read once for the run with its maps downscaled to the tier's texture size (each of the pack's maps read once by file name, normal and roughness maps not at all), a person's fit is built in steps under the frame budget and kept for the next time, everybody wears one wardrobe of materials whose colours, panels and coverage are uniforms read per draw off the person drawn (a skin per body map, a tint per hair and eye map, panels per garment), and `HeroCharacter.prepare(onProgress)` reads and prepares both crowd bodies and warms their programs with every garment's at load, so a conversation, a fall or a laid-out body links nothing.
- A close person who is talked to hands their rig to the talk with nothing built, and when the talk ends while they are still close the rig stays theirs, following their body again; talked to out of reach, the rig goes with the talk. A new focused rig starts in the crowd body's clip at the crowd's baked frame as they are when it takes the slot, so the swap shows no pose change: its first segment blends from there over the segment's blend, or plays on when it is that same loop. The caller leaves that body's clip as it is until `show` resolves, so an entry (crouch, sit, sprint) plays whole from the pose the body stood in. A loop the rig is asked for again plays on from where it is; a one-shot starts over.
- Speech gesture: the rig has no jaw bone or face morph targets, so speech shows in the head. While the focused person's voice plays, turns about the root's side, up and forward are layered on `neck_01` (40%) and `Head` (60%) after the animation mixer, so the talk clip plays under them, and the bones are handed back to the clip before the mixer's next update. They are a nod when the loudness outruns the loudest of late by a quarter (a stressed word, or the first after a pause; 0.4 s, 2.1 to 3.9 degrees down, at least 0.45 s apart), a dip with each syllable and a slow sway of two sines per axis. Each axis follows its aim as a critically damped spring (0.15 s) and stays within 4 degrees of pitch, 5 of yaw and 2.5 of roll by a soft bound. The sway's rates and phases and each nod's size come from the line's seed, so a line heard from rest moves the same way each time. When the voice ends the head eases back to the clip over about 2 s and is then left alone. Seated and standing rigs move alike. The brows stay: the pack's Eyebrows mesh carries the eyelashes on the same Head weights.
- Scheduled and follow movement samples only Connections `path3`; flat compatibility paths never position a body.
- Walkers of one reported group stand at least 1.2 m apart along their lane, keep their own side of it through turns, and come no nearer than 0.6 m to another body, at spawn and walking. A group its segment cannot hold carries on to the next one. Each walks at its own pace between 0.9 and 1.3 m/s, on its own footfall.
- A walker looks 2.5 m ahead along its line for anybody in it: another body, walking, standing, held by a conversation or walked by continuity, and the player's feet the crowd is updated with. It steps across the pavement at 0.8 m/s, out to the pavement's edge less its radius (0.3 m where a stretch publishes no width), to the nearest line that passes everybody there at 0.6 m, or at 0.66 m from the player (the pushback's reach, so a walker never shoves the player); meeting somebody coming the other way it keeps to its own right. Until it is clear it walks on no nearer than that and, with nowhere to step, turns back before somebody standing, or stands behind somebody walking for at most 2 s before it turns back, unless they wait at a crossing. Somebody walking the same way at least as fast is not in its line. A walker inside somebody's space, or the player's, gives way across the pavement as well as along it, to the side with more room when they are straight ahead or behind. A body continuity walks down the street is drawn stepped around whoever is in its way the same way, and back onto its line once nobody is near; continuity's own position and pace are left as they are.
- A car eases down for anybody in its lane within 8 m, standing, walking or lying, and holds 1.5 m short. Only somebody who steps in inside its braking distance is hit.
- A fall ends when the body stops moving, or after six seconds. The person then stands up and walks on from where they came to rest, or, where the simulation has buried them, leaves the crowd and is one of that pavement's numbers again.
- A walker on a street edge (sidewalk, access, crossing) stands on the top of the cover under their feet (`surface.height`); where none lies, sidewalk and access grade add `surface.paving` and a crossing keeps 0.02 m clearance. A continuity actor on a walk edge is drawn the same way from its projected grade, so a body continuity walks at grade, or one a conversation left on the pavement, stands on the pavement. Continuity positions inside buildings and at stops are used as published.
- Scheduled passenger transit uses the routine's exact route, board stop, alight stop and progress. Ordered duplicate stops select the shortest forward portion of the route shape, so return legs keep their direction and heading.
- Follow speed is bounded and its stopping distance is deterministic. Explicit stop does not teleport the visible actor to its schedule, and starting control does not teleport a visible body to its schedule.
- Lead speed and pacing depend only on the saved route, the player position, the simulation time and the pace. Passenger carry requires the exact active follower, route id and a successful non-fallen body projection.
- Idle, walk, sprint and seated states select the corresponding audited clip. A body standing or sitting still rests in the idle variety of its role (`IdleVariety.js`): a guard, a clerk, a worker, anybody else and a seated guest each rest on a loop of their role at a pace of their own (0.86 to 1.14 of the clip's) from a phase of their own, and now and then play one of their role's fidgets through once (looking about and rubbing the neck, reaching for something at the post, a drink; seated, a hand to the head, a nod along, a sip), all decided by their appearance seed and role. - An interior waiter, barista or vendor keeps the simulation type chosen for that post. The engine does not reinterpret an interior role as an unrelated type.

## Boundary behavior

- The gameplay animation coordinator owns speaking and listening gestures. This controller publishes the exact identity, posture, follow mode, and routine resume state it consumes. A waiting or arrived leader is `idle`, turned toward the player. The coordinator presents a leader as it presents a follower: the follow walk or sprint action while it moves, idle while it stands, and the same action when it changes mode where it stands.
- Simulation route workers publish a route workplace but no trip assignment. They fail closed because no authoritative vehicle position or route progress exists; passenger commute legs carry the required transit assignment.

## Dialogue ownership

A statistical sample carrying an established `npcId` reuses the canonical named body. A sampled handle already owned by a quest/continuity actor cannot spawn another anonymous copy; later identity resolution also merges a pre-existing alias. Stress copies never inherit persistent identity: they carry no handle and a look of their own, and a copy or a retiring body is talked to as nobody. During dialogue, continuity owns position and the animation director keeps the standing/seated posture, speaking and listening clips while rejecting schedule locomotion. Ending a conversation releases the interruption or retains the next quest appointment; released, the person first lingers turned to the player. The host closes a restored conversation when its UI was not restored.

## Conversation placement and scheduled posts

The clicked body supplies position, facing, parcel and seated posture before handle identification. If it resolves to an older canonical body, that same canonical object adopts the clicked placement rather than moving the conversation across the city. Active escorts and explicitly controlled aliases cannot be relocated this way.

A stationary parcel conversation may carry `post:{heading,spot?}` in its start request. Continuity preserves the visible position and idle/sit posture in optional saved `posts` records keyed by npcId and the absolute schedule occurrence (entry index, start/end and schedule place). Closing resumes Simulation, but the worker or visitor stays at that post while the occurrence is unchanged. A real schedule transition expires the placement; it is separate from quest holds and follow ownership. Optional actor `spot` restores the exact Crowd reservation after streaming, and is cleared on physical relocation.

Legacy saved conversations without a post may have no usable return path from their actual interior position. Closing such a parcel conversation keeps that visible position for the current schedule occurrence instead of throwing, leaving stale conversation control, or deleting the person on the next frame.

Room-fill sampling binds the currently drawn mesh’s channel per object, so a cached focused model never retains the disposed channel from warm-up or a previous person. Channel replacement and growth update both the sampled texture and its texel dimensions.
