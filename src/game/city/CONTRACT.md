# City geometry

Loads assembled shells and fixtures and streams furnished interior floors, drawn from room modules batched by material, into the game scene.

Building and door entries follow the [game contract](../CONTRACT.md). This contract specifies interior and street fixture streams.

`Venues({ atlas, buildings, doors, fixtures, factory, signs })` ([Venues.js](Venues.js)) names and marks the places the player can go into. Every door gets a header fixture; a venue's sign fixtures follow who the simulation has on duty. `marks` lists every enterable venue type for the minimap, plus the parcel the active objective points at whatever its type, at its door or, unbuilt, its access point. `nameOf(parcelId)` is what the objective calls a parcel: the word on its building (a shell's lettered sign, or the `word` a kit parcel letters on its plan's field), else a word for its type. `setObjective({ parcelId, name } | null)` sets that parcel and, when the questline names the place and the building's sign field carries no word of its own, letters that name on it through the kit sign batch; it answers whether the marks changed.

## Input

- `StreetMarkings.build(atlas, networks, factory, resolver, mode, native=false)` returns a marking group. With `native:true`, the saved bundle owns ordinary paint and this builder retains highway paint only. Explicit `debug` or `glow` modes still show authoritative lane diagnostics.

- [Stream schema](schema/interior-stream.d.ts): constructor ports, registration, player feet and callbacks.
- `InteriorModules({catalog, baseUrl, factory, roomLights, loader?, readBinary?, only?})` takes Interior's [shared module catalog](../../../../interior/schemas/modules.schema.json), the directory its GLBs sit in and the [room light pool](../light/CONTRACT.md) its slot materials are lit by. `only` is the set of module ids to read, `placedModules(buildings)`: every module the floors of the buildings the interior stream registers place, window returns and lifts included, or null (the whole catalog) when a floor cannot be read, which leaves the error to the stream. A game passes it, so a city reads the room vocabulary its buildings use, not the catalog's. `ready` resolves when every module asked for is standing; a module not read is one no registered floor places.
- `InteriorProps({catalog, baseUrl, roomLights, loadAsset?})` takes Interior's furniture catalog, the directory its `modelUri` files sit in and the same pool. `prepare(ids)` resolves when those models are standing; an id the catalog does not publish, a local-only model the machine lacks, stands nowhere and the first request naming it warns once. `placedProps(buildings)` names every furniture id the floors of the registered buildings place, and a game reads those models with the room catalogs, so their draws are prepared with the load and a floor coming into sight brings no model, map or program to the frame; one that fails to read is left to the floor that places it. `hold(copies)` takes, per furniture id, the most copies the city stands at once: a draw is born with room for them, rounded up to a power of two of at least 64 (`roomFor`), and one already standing moves to that room now, so a draw never grows on the frame a floor comes into sight; a draw of one capacity shares its programs with every other of that capacity, because the shader holds the instance buffer's length.
- Per furnished parcel, `interior/building.json` ([schema](../../../../interior/schemas/building.schema.json)) and the layouts it names ([schema](../../../../interior/schemas/floor-placement.schema.json)), as `{building, layouts}` on the building source.
- `BuildingsLoader(factory, loader?, options?, slice?, hitches?).load(buildings)` also consumes optional Exterior `modelInstances`, with [building model settings and outputs](schema/building-models.d.ts). It reads a shell one surface at a time, window scenery included, asking `slice` (the [frame budget](../../app/FrameBudget.js)) between them, so a landmark admitted while the city is drawn costs a step and not a building; each surface read and each material merged is named in `hitches` (`shell surface`, `shell merge`, a [HitchLog](../debug/CONTRACT.md)). Every mesh node the shell publishes is drawn, bucketed by the material it wears, and collides where that material does: a node's name answers for its moving leaves and for nothing else, because a producer names its nodes for its own reasons and a family signs itself with parts named after it. The [asset mapping](building-models.json) chooses installed Props catalog models for each vegetation kind. Beside `doors` and `entrances` it returns `closedEntrances`: for every shell with no interior, its street entrance as `closedEntrance(blueprint)` ([DoorGeometry.js](DoorGeometry.js)) reads it, the main ground-floor door, shopfront or aperture, whose leaves stay baked into the shell and solid.
- A layout's `floor` follows [Interior's floor schema](../../../../interior/schemas/floor.schema.json), measured from that floor's own walking surface.
- `StreetLamps(atlas, factory, walk?)` takes the Game contract's Atlas world, material factory and optional Connections walk graph. `.stream(options?)` follows the [street fixture schema](schema/street-fixtures.d.ts).
- `ShellSurface.js` answers what one shell surface wears, for the city and the [building preview](../../building/CONTRACT.md) alike: `shellVariant(factory, {key, authored, blueprint, parcelId})`, `shellMaterial(factory, {key, variantId, doubleSided, exterior})` (an `exterior` surface, which the kit and shell loaders ask for and room modules do not, wears the factory's [surface-detail](../surface-detail/CONTRACT.md) wear by its profile where the run lays one), `isSceneryNode(node)` and `shellScenery(node, factory, {key, scenic, cells})`, which answers a lit window room baked on each grid in `cells` (a metre alone by default) in one pass, and any other scenery surface once.
- `StoreyPlates.js` is what a building that opens an interior does with Exterior's `floor:<index>/slab` plates: `storeyIndex(node)` names the storey a node is the plate of, `interiorStoreys(parcelId, interior)` is the rectangle each furnished floor's rooms and vertical core fill in its `coreAngleDeg` frame at that floor's elevation, and `cutPlate(geometry, rect)` returns the part of a plate outside that rotated rectangle, preserving the original world vertices and attributes. Core rectangles rotate about their published centres with that frame. Every plate of a furnished building is cut by its own floor's rectangle: the band between the rectangle and the outline stays drawn and solid, because that band is open floor the interior does not reach, and the rectangle itself, wells included, is given up to the module floors so nothing is drawn twice and the stair and lift openings are real holes. A floor the interior does not publish, a basement under it for instance, keeps its plate whole, and so does every plate of a closed building.
- `ShellFixtures.js`: `shellGlows({parcelId, blueprint, hasInterior, rng?})` returns every fixture a building carries itself, in lumens, and `parcelSeed(parcelId)` is the seeded stream its signs are drawn from.

## Output

An Exterior opening with `scenery` references its authored `scenery:<floor>` GLB node. Shells render those nodes with their published materials and skip generated room replacements for those openings. Buildings selected for real interiors keep that same exterior appearance. `ExteriorScenery(blueprint)` masks their scenic surfaces while the camera is inside any of the building's floor volumes, using each floor's outline, elevation and height. Its camera-driven uniform belongs to the render group so static meshes update after entering and leaving. Real interior materials remain unchanged, views out remain open, and neighboring buildings keep their scenery. Scenic geometry creates no collision; paired cladding remains solid shell geometry.

A surface variant is the one authored on it, else the one its building published for that key, else the pattern seeded by its parcel. A key containing `/light-fixture/` is a lit diffuser: it is built at emissive level 180 at 2700 K instead of its catalog strength.

Fallback window scenes use one rectangular box per window, one metre deep behind the published glazing plane, with one rear image and ceiling lighting. Furnished parcels use the same exterior-only camera mask. Unfittable boxes are omitted. Window dimensions remain unchanged.

Optional facade accent light color, flux and range override entrance defaults. Opaque black glazing, garden concrete, corporate panels, ivory panels, facade chrome and cast exterior concrete retain shell collision. Authored scenery and imported plant instances stay outside that collision path regardless of material.

Building vegetation preserves imported geometry, textures and proportions, with its original root at the authored position and yaw. Uniform scaling fits the whole model inside its local size box. Material parts are instanced per source model in each loaded shell group. These decorative plants add no collision. Pine supplies tall planting requested as ornamental trees or palms; scaled maple supplies shrubs. A null asset mapping omits that kind and reports it in `unresolvedModelInstances`. Configured assets that fail loading reject with `E_PROP_ASSET`; malformed placements or unknown configured assets reject with `E_BUILDING_MODEL`. Cell release calls `disposeModelInstances()` to release instance buffers, source geometry, materials and images after admission has settled.

Optional `scenery.lights` entries publish actual fixture positions, colors, lumens and finite ranges. Neon passes them into the shared CityLights pool for closed shells. Opening IDs identify their sources; dark rooms emit zero lumens. Source positions and fixture geometry share one placement calculation.

Paired scenic receiver surfaces carry a load-time `scenicRadiance` RGB vertex attribute. The original planes and UVs are subdivided on a metre grid. Downward fixture illumination and diffuse floor return are evaluated inside the owning room, independently of player position or live light-slot selection. Ceilings receive diffuse return. The material multiplies its original albedo by that field and the shared night switch; live point lights do not relight these static receivers. Geometry merges by material binding and receiver shading across closed buildings; each furnished building merges its own scenery under its camera mask. Ordinary surfaces sharing a room material retain ordinary shading in a separate batch. Legacy surfaces without emitter records keep their catalog emission level. Scenic materials are cell-owned; catalog maps remain factory-owned.

`InteriorModules` publishes one batch per material slot for the whole city, in `group`, holding every module surface that wears it. A slot is `key#variant` as the catalog publishes it: the GLB material is named by the key and carries the variant in `userData.materialVariant`, and a mesh whose material name is not a key falls back to the slot published at its position. A slot material is built the way a shell surface is (`shellMaterial`) from the factory's `key` and `variantId`, so a `/light-fixture/` slot is a lit diffuser at emissive level 180 at 2700 K in the variant the GLB names, and every slot material wears the room light pool. Module UVs are tile units, one unit per repeat of the slot's map; the slot material tiles world metres and is shared with the shells, so each module surface's UVs are divided by that material's texture repeat once at load and the map lands exactly as often as Interior authored it, with no further tiling transform. `batchCount` is the number of published slots and admitting a floor never adds to it; `copyCount` is how many module copies stand. A copy is one instance per surface, naming the geometry that surface was registered as, the copy's world matrix and the fill of the room it stands in (`admit(id, matrix, fill)`), and a floor grows the batches it will touch once, from its own placement table, before it appends anything. Registering the buildings sizes the module batches and the furniture draws once (`hold`, [InteriorPeaks.js](InteriorPeaks.js)) for the most copies the stream can stand at once around any one place: per batch or furniture model, over every building, what the buildings drawn together with it hold (within twice the 36 m its floors stay drawn) at their fullest window of three floors, plus one more window for a lift's destination, plus the slabs the open buildings (within twice 95 m) stand under floors not shown yet. A floor coming into sight then appends into room its draws already have, and the load prepares them at that size; a floor the peak missed still grows a batch in place. Room batches cast only into the room light's shadow on tiers that have one, their copies are frustum tested one by one, and they do not sort. A module whose positions the producer quantized is dequantized before its node transform is folded in, so a module taller than its quantized range stands at its published size. `boundsOf`, `slotsOf` and `surfacesOf` answer for one module. `InteriorProps` publishes one draw per model material part, each part wearing its own material through the room light pool, standing on the floor with its footprint centred and scaled uniformly to the height its catalog publishes, which the catalog measures as the third entry of `dimensionsMeters`; a copy carries its room's fill the same way (`admit(id, matrix, fill)`).

`group`, `rooms`, `liveInteriors`, `floorShown(parcelId, floor)`, `update(feet)` and collider callbacks follow the stream schema. `floorShown` is true while that floor is solid and admitted, drawn whenever its building is in sight, which is when a staged scene on it may stand. A floor that comes into the draws, shown or seen again, puts its copies in two milliseconds of each `update` at a time (`update(feet, { paint })`), oldest floor first and its own slabs first, and its placeholder slabs stand until those are in; `prepare` puts them all in at once. Registration measures the building footprint, opens within 70 m and drops past 95 m. A building's admitted floors are drawn while its footprint is within 32 m of the player on the ground, the reach of the room view, and leave the draws past 36 m; beyond that they stay built and solid, so walking in never waits for them. Interior draws cast into the room light's shadow only while the player stands in a building (within 1 m of its footprint) or has asked its lift for a floor. Two builds and two admissions have independent budgets, nearest building first. A build given the game's [frame budget](../../app/FrameBudget.js) (`new InteriorStream({ budget })`) asks it between its steps (the rooms and their fills, the apartment doors, the copies, the haze, the cuboids), so no floor holds the thread for its whole build in one frame, and one that stops being wanted at a step lets go of what it had built there. `prepare(feet)` admits nearby floors during startup. `requestFloor(parcelId, floor)` pins a lift destination and its neighbours until `releaseFloor(parcelId)`, and `floorFailed(parcelId, floor)` says whether that floor failed to build or to stand solid, which is not retried. Real module slabs stay drawn and solid while furnishing prepares. Entrance doors wait for `floorShown` before opening. A floor is its layout's placements at the building floor's elevation: a `module` placement becomes a copy of that module draw, a `prop` placement a copy of that furniture draw, and both keep the layout's `room` and `connector`. A copy carries the fill of the room its placement names, or the floor's rooms taken together when it names none. Floors with equal construction reuse a layout and differ only by elevation. A changing landmark floor may name its own `floor-N` layout; the runtime reads each floor’s declared table, including its own rooms, core, fixtures and placements. The player's floor and its neighbors are solid, and drawn while their building is in sight; one further floor on either side keeps its table without a copy in any draw. Dropping a floor removes exactly its copies. A layout naming a module the catalog lacks fails that floor with `E_INTERIOR_MODULE`; a furniture model the catalog publishes but cannot load fails with `E_INTERIOR_PROP`, while a `prop` placement naming furniture the catalog does not publish stands empty; a building that names a layout it does not carry fails with `E_INTERIOR_LAYOUT`. A world published without a furniture catalog stands unfurnished.

Each floor's own renderables, its lift leaves and its haze, prepare one at a time through `Warmup.warmAll` before it is drawn; the module batches and the furniture draws are prepared once with the scene, which is one compile per slot rather than one per module surface. Cancellation is checked between renderables.

`onColliderBand` receives `{boxes, positions}`: one cuboid per solid module part and catalog furniture placement; `positions` is empty. Furniture dimensions are catalog width, depth and height, transformed by placement scale, yaw and floor elevation. Modules are cuboids only, never trimesh, and which are solid follows the id prefixes Interior publishes: `wall-panel-*`, `wall-field-*`, `wall-light-line*`, `floor-*`, `ceiling-band-*`, `ceiling-field-*` and the fitted furniture `fit-*` are their published bounds, a door frame is two jambs and a lintel so the doorway is a real hole, a window return is its four reveals, and a stair flight is its run of tread cuboids and the exact guard posts on both edges. The post spacing blocks a player from crossing the visible rail while the flight ends remain open onto their turn landings. `ceiling-spot*`, `ceiling-cove-*`, `ceiling-led-strip`, `ceiling-services`, `wall-screen`, `wall-art`, `wall-shelf` and the lift car and landing leaves carry no collision. Every part is cut out of the module's published `size` and `origin`, so re-authored modules carry their colliders with them; a part that would fall outside those bounds fails the floor with `E_INTERIOR_MODULE`. Its readiness promise keeps the floor out of the draws while Physics prepares. A floor leaving the visible window calls `onDropBand`, including during admission; stale readiness cannot show it. Collision errors release the floor and report its identifier. Builds and collision admissions progress independently. Dropped or cancelled floors release their temporary slabs too.

`Elevators.mount(parcelId, floor, placement, modules, group)` binds the published `lift-car`, `lift-car-doors`, `lift-car-head` and `lift-doors` placements, and a through car's own (`LIFT_MODULES`), to the shaft standing at them, the shaft the placement's `connector` names first and the nearest one after: the car is the module standing in the group that rides, the car's own front rides with it at the pose its placements give, its leaves split at their zero and hung on sliders, and a landing's leaves are that module split at its own zero and hung on sliders, with the call plate and its button beside them. The car's leaves run with the landing's it stands at and are shut whenever it is between floors, so nothing of the shaft is seen from the car, and the cab waits for them to shut before it moves; a car published without its own front (an older world, such as one whose floors place only `lift-car` and `lift-doors`) takes leaves cut from a landing's, as wide as its doorway, running just ahead of its front under a plain head up to its ceiling, whichever of car and landing a floor mounts first, and gives way to the published front when a floor carries one. The car's front is lit as the car is, by its own lens through the car materials every shaft shares. A through car (`lift-car-through`, an Interior composed core's) is open at both ends: it carries a back pair of leaves and a back head as well (`lift-car-rear-doors`, `lift-car-rear-head`), which ride in the cab like the front ones and are shut whenever it is between floors; its back pair opens, with the front one, only at a floor that publishes `lift-rear-doors`, whose back landing slides, seals (`lift:<parcel>:<shaft>@<floor>:rear`) and holds the car for a body in its doorway as the front landing does, and the waiting car stands no back wall, only the cheeks of its back doorway. Those copies never enter the shared draws. A core rect is read by its minimum corner. Shaft membership, passenger containment and the waiting car's colliders follow `coreAngleDeg` and the mounted car's yaw and scale, oblique cores included. Interior's `LIFT_CAR` spec ([lift-spec.ts](../../../../interior/src/geometry/lift-spec.ts)) gives the car's floor, walls, ceiling, lens and panel coordinates, scaled with the car placement. Travel is 1.6 to 6 m/s according to shaft height, reached and left at 1.5 m/s² so the cab levels at a floor. The cab waits for shut doors before moving and for a solid destination before opening. A rider carried to a floor that `floorFailed` reports, or that has not stood for 45 s, is taken back to the floor they set off from when that one is shown, and let out there. A rider enters the PlayerBody carry mode at a fixed cab offset, then regains capsule collision after the destination and cab are solid. A shaft stands its own cuboids through the stream's collider callbacks, under ids of its own (`lift:<parcel>:<shaft>/cab` and `lift:<parcel>:<shaft>@<floor>`): a landing is solid while its leaves are shut and until they have run fully open, and the waiting car stands its floor, both side walls, back wall, roof and the two cheeks beside its door, gone while it travels because a rider is then being carried. A body standing in a landing's doorway holds its leaves open and the cab where it is, a jump included. A car's surfaces are lit by its own lens and a bounce and by nothing of the city's, and a landing's leaves by that lens beside their room lights. Every car and every landing wears one material per source for the run, and every display the same two strokes in one material ([CabLight.js](CabLight.js)): the lens a surface is lit by is read per draw from the shaft the drawn mesh names, so no shaft's car or landing builds a graph of its own, and a building let go and opened again builds none either. `specimen(modules)` is one of each of those draws, detached, for the load's warm-up to prepare before any shaft stands, and `discard(specimen)` lets go of the geometry it cut for itself; the stream also hands a floor's warm-up the cars its shafts have stood (`cars(parcelId)`), which finds them prepared. A building's cars are drawn while its floors are (`draw(parcelId, drawn)`, the same 32 m sight as the floors). A shaft whose building is let go drops its colliders, its landings' leaf geometry and what it cut or built for its car's front; the shared materials and strokes stay.

E on a landing's call button calls the cab; a call made while it travels waits until it has arrived and stood unattended for four seconds. Inside, E acts on the car panel's button under the crosshair, each a target where the model draws it that the crosshair ray has to pass within 6 cm of: the top row chooses the next or previous served floor, the middle row opens or closes the doors, the bottom row travels to the chosen floor or resets the choice to the floor the car stands at. Page Up and Page Down choose anywhere inside the car, without wrapping. A display of drawn segments on the panel shows the chosen floor, basements and skipped floor numbers included. A landing let go and built again while its open cab waits there runs its leaves open again.

A world assembled with an older car is read at the size its own catalog publishes for `lift-car`: the spec's floor, walls and roof scale to it, and the front cheeks, panel and display that car never had are left out, so E anywhere in it travels to the chosen floor as it did before the spec.

Each car owns a warm area light at its lens and a bounce light, which ride with it. They stay out of the scene's light list and light only the car's cloned materials, and each landing's leaves wear clones that add that car's area light to the room pool. An area light shades with LTC tables that tiers without room strips never install, so the car installs them itself (`installAreaLights`, [Light](../light/CONTRACT.md)) and is lit alike on every tier and backend. The car's materials keep the shared model's opaque surfaces.

Interior publishes each floor's private apartment entrances beside its layout, in `building.floors[].apartmentEntrances`, never as placements, and InteriorLayouts copies them to that floor's record, since numbers and ownership differ between floors that share a layout. A floor's build stands each entrance's two pocket leaves and fixed track, end stops and numberplate as copies of their modules with the scale baked into the vertices and the maps kept in metres, lit by the fill of the corridor and the home they stand between in a channel of the door's own; none of them enters the floor's static boxes or the shared draws, and the floor disposes them with the rest of its content. An entrance that is not a pair of pocket leaves in a doorway of 0.7 to 2.4 m by 2.1 to 3 m, or names a module the catalog does not publish, is refused with `E_APARTMENT_DOOR`: `buildApartmentDoors` throws it, and the interior stream, which passes `refused`, warns and leaves that doorway open while the rest of the floor stands, so one door never fails a floor. `placedModules` names every entrance's leaf and fixed modules, so a game reads them with the rest.

`ApartmentDoors(physics)` makes a shown floor's apartment doors doors: each leaf collides as its own triangles through DoorColliders, and the Interactor offers them to E and runs them beside the street doors, aimed at handle height above the floor-level centre they publish. They never join `city.doors`, a building's entrances, a parcel's street access, anybody's street goals or Exterior's openings. Hiding a floor takes its doors' bodies out and remembers how each was left; a floor shown again, or built again, stands them that way until its building is let go. The engine never fabricates the walls, cassettes or privacy closures around them, which Interior publishes as solid modules, nor widens a doorway. A floor record without apartment entrances loads as before.

`buildingFloors` also gives each floor record its `roomEntrances` ([RoomDoors.js](RoomDoors.js)): a door for each doorway into a private room the [access](../access/CONTRACT.md) addresses number (an office, executive office, kitchen behind a counter, archive, store, plant, security or server room), which Interior publishes as an open framed passage. Each is an entrance in the form an apartment's takes (`role: 'room'`, its `scope` and the room's address `label`): a pair of the building's own pocket leaves (the kit its apartment doors wear, else its tier's), each scaled to half the doorway at Interior's door head (2.5 m, 3 m for three leaves or more, a casing under the ceiling), closing in the wall line and sliding along it into the wall, with no track, stops or plate. `buildApartmentDoors` stands them after the floor's apartment entrances and ApartmentDoors makes them doors alike, so they collide, lock by their scope, open by E and close by themselves; `placedModules` names their leaf module. Nothing is published for them, so an existing world stands them as it loads.

A published room is its footprint, the fixtures the interior hung in it, and the surfaces its modules stand as. Its fill light is computed from the surface it encloses, measured from its own outline: floor and ceiling, plus a wall around the outline and around every hole in it, at the storey's height. The modules standing in it, every wall, floor, ceiling, frame, return and stair one, weight the reflectance at their placed size and the mean of the slots they wear, with `floor-*` modules as the walked surface; lit modules, hung pieces, fitted furniture and the lift are not surface. A module's bounding box counts backs buried in walls and faces its neighbours hide, which comes to about twice the room's real enclosing surface and up to six times in a corridor, so those areas weight reflectance and never stand as the room's own area. A light record addressed to a room the floor does not publish, a stair core above all, joins the floor's shared fill and its haze instead of being dropped, and the run names the rooms it could not find. Every light record published for the room reaches its fixtures, the `cove` joints at a wall's top and foot included, and a record carrying a `furniture` id keeps it, naming the placement whose module carries the lens. Rooms own no geometry: `RoomView` marks the ones in reach and on the player's level `visible`, nearest first. Reach is measured to a room's own extent on the ground and never to one anchor in the middle of it, because a sales floor 26 m across and a mall floor 53 m across would otherwise drop out of view, and with them their light, their air and their exposure, the moment the player walked away from the middle.

`StreetLamps.build()` returns the complete `{group, posts, glows, dispose}` scene. `.stream()` returns nearby instances, source-derived whole-plan post reservations and admission ports in the street fixture schema. Source planning yields across frames and preserves source order: authored median poles, 19 m alternating posts, widest junction corners, 34 m plaza borders, 6 m separation, complete furnishing bases and overhead clearances. Pole bases also exclude Connections walking ribbons, Atlas crossing landings and reserved walking bands; the kerb search accepts only a fully clear seat. Uncovered walk legs receive wall-mounted fixtures on nearby facing facades. Spatial queries retain every overlapping authored volume.

Street fixtures belong to exactly one half-open cell by their final mounting position, independent of movement order. Cells are 128 m by default (16-256 m supported). Only nearby cells instantiate original pole, head, lens and wall-pack templates. New cells prepare rendering and requested collision before visibility. Later ports also prepare resident cells. Eviction cancels pending posts and releases instance buffers; return restores the same fixtures and glow identities. Disposal releases template geometry; factory materials stay shared. Invalid windows reject with `E_STREET_FIXTURE_WINDOW`; preparation and collision errors propagate after releasing the failed cell.
