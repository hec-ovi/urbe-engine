# City geometry

Loads assembled shells and fixtures and streams furnished interior floors, drawn from room modules batched by material, into the game scene.

Building and door entries follow the [game contract](../CONTRACT.md). This contract specifies interior and street fixture streams.

## Input

- `StreetMarkings.build(atlas, networks, factory, resolver, mode, native=false)` returns a marking group. With `native:true`, the saved bundle owns ordinary paint and this builder retains highway paint only. Explicit `debug` or `glow` modes still show authoritative lane diagnostics.

- [Stream schema](schema/interior-stream.d.ts): constructor ports, registration, player feet and callbacks.
- `InteriorModules({catalog, baseUrl, factory, loader?, readBinary?})` takes Interior's [shared module catalog](../../../../interior/schemas/modules.schema.json) and the directory its GLBs sit in. `ready` resolves when every module is standing.
- `InteriorProps({catalog, baseUrl, loadAsset?})` takes Interior's furniture catalog and the directory its `modelUri` files sit in. `prepare(ids)` resolves when those models are standing.
- Per furnished parcel, `interior/building.json` ([schema](../../../../interior/schemas/building.schema.json)) and the three layouts it names ([schema](../../../../interior/schemas/floor-placement.schema.json)), as `{building, layouts}` on the building source.
- `BuildingsLoader(factory, loader?, options?, slice?).load(buildings)` also consumes optional Exterior `modelInstances`, with [building model settings and outputs](schema/building-models.d.ts). It reads a shell one surface at a time, window scenery included, asking `slice` (the [frame budget](../../app/FrameBudget.js)) between them, so a landmark admitted while the city is drawn costs a step and not a building. Every mesh node the shell publishes is drawn, bucketed by the material it wears, and collides where that material does: a node's name answers for its moving leaves and for nothing else, because a producer names its nodes for its own reasons and a family signs itself with parts named after it. The [asset mapping](building-models.json) chooses installed Props catalog models for each vegetation kind.
- A layout's `floor` follows [Interior's floor schema](../../../../interior/schemas/floor.schema.json), measured from that floor's own walking surface.
- `StreetLamps(atlas, factory, walk?)` takes the Game contract's Atlas world, material factory and optional Connections walk graph. `.stream(options?)` follows the [street fixture schema](schema/street-fixtures.d.ts).
- `ShellSurface.js` answers what one shell surface wears, for the city and the [building preview](../../building/CONTRACT.md) alike: `shellVariant(factory, {key, authored, blueprint, parcelId})`, `shellMaterial(factory, {key, variantId, doubleSided})`, `isSceneryNode(node)` and `shellScenery(node, factory, {key, hasInterior, scenic})`.
- `ShellFixtures.js`: `shellGlows({parcelId, blueprint, hasInterior, rng?})` returns every fixture a building carries itself, in lumens, and `parcelSeed(parcelId)` is the seeded stream its signs are drawn from.

## Output

An Exterior opening with `scenery` references its authored `scenery:<floor>` GLB node. Closed shells render those nodes with their published materials and skip generated room replacements for those openings. Buildings selected for real interiors omit the scenic nodes. Scenic geometry creates no collision; paired cladding remains solid shell geometry.

A surface variant is the one authored on it, else the one its building published for that key, else the pattern seeded by its parcel. A key containing `/light-fixture/` is a lit diffuser: it is built at emissive level 180 at 2700 K instead of its catalog strength.

Fallback window scenes use one rectangular box per window, one metre deep behind the published glazing plane, with one rear image and ceiling lighting. Unfittable boxes are omitted. Window dimensions remain unchanged.

Optional facade accent light color, flux and range override entrance defaults. Opaque black glazing, garden concrete, corporate panels, ivory panels, facade chrome and cast exterior concrete retain shell collision. Authored scenery and imported plant instances stay outside that collision path regardless of material.

Building vegetation preserves imported geometry, textures and proportions, with its original root at the authored position and yaw. Uniform scaling fits the whole model inside its local size box. Material parts are instanced per source model in each loaded shell group. These decorative plants add no collision. Pine supplies tall planting requested as ornamental trees or palms; scaled maple supplies shrubs. A null asset mapping omits that kind and reports it in `unresolvedModelInstances`. Configured assets that fail loading reject with `E_PROP_ASSET`; malformed placements or unknown configured assets reject with `E_BUILDING_MODEL`. Cell release calls `disposeModelInstances()` to release instance buffers, source geometry, materials and images after admission has settled.

Optional `scenery.lights` entries publish actual fixture positions, colors, lumens and finite ranges. Neon passes them into the shared CityLights pool for closed shells. Opening IDs identify their sources; dark rooms emit zero lumens. Source positions and fixture geometry share one placement calculation.

Paired scenic receiver surfaces carry a load-time `scenicRadiance` RGB vertex attribute. The original planes and UVs are subdivided on a metre grid. Downward fixture illumination and diffuse floor return are evaluated inside the owning room, independently of player position or live light-slot selection. Ceilings receive diffuse return. The material multiplies its original albedo by that field and the shared night switch; live point lights do not relight these static receivers. Geometry merges by material binding and receiver shading across all loaded buildings. Ordinary surfaces sharing a room material retain ordinary shading in a separate batch. Legacy surfaces without emitter records keep their catalog emission level. Scenic materials are cell-owned; catalog maps remain factory-owned.

`InteriorModules` publishes one batch per material slot for the whole city, in `group`, holding every module surface that wears it: seventeen modules wearing twenty-seven surfaces over six slots are six draws. `batchCount` is that number and admitting a floor never adds to it; `copyCount` is how many module copies stand. A copy is one instance per surface, naming the geometry that surface was registered as and the copy's world matrix, and a floor grows the batches it will touch once, from its own placement table, before it appends anything. Room batches do not cast shadows, their copies are frustum tested one by one, and they do not sort. A module whose positions the producer quantized is dequantized before its node transform is folded in, so a module taller than its quantized range stands at its published size. `boundsOf`, `slotsOf` and `surfacesOf` answer for one module. `InteriorProps` publishes one draw per model material part, standing on the floor with its footprint centred and scaled uniformly to the height its catalog publishes, which the catalog measures as the third entry of `dimensionsMeters`.

`group`, `rooms`, `liveInteriors`, `update(feet)` and collider callbacks follow the stream schema. Registration opens buildings within 70 m and drops them past 95 m. The nearest requested floor builds first, one at a time. A floor is its layout's placements at the building floor's elevation: a `module` placement becomes a copy of that module draw, a `prop` placement a copy of that furniture draw, and both keep the layout's `room` and `connector`. Every middle floor of a building reads one layout table and differs only by elevation. The player's floor and its neighbors are drawn and solid; one further floor on either side keeps its table without a copy in any draw. Dropping a floor removes exactly its copies. A layout naming a module the catalog lacks fails that floor with `E_INTERIOR_MODULE`; a furniture id or model the catalog cannot load fails with `E_INTERIOR_PROP`; a building that names a layout it does not carry fails with `E_INTERIOR_LAYOUT`. A world published without a furniture catalog stands unfurnished.

Each floor's own renderables, its lift leaves and its haze, prepare one at a time through `Warmup.warmAll` before it is drawn; the module batches and the furniture draws are prepared once with the scene, which is one compile per slot rather than one per module surface. Cancellation is checked between renderables.

`onColliderBand` receives `{boxes, positions}`: one cuboid per solid module part, and the borrowed world-space triangles of the floor's furniture. Modules are cuboids only, never trimesh: a wall segment, slab or ceiling tile is its published bounds, a door frame is two jambs and a lintel so the doorway is a real hole, a window return is its four reveals, and a stair flight is its run of tread cuboids. Every part is cut out of the module's published `size` and `origin`, so re-authored modules carry their colliders with them; a part that would fall outside those bounds fails the floor with `E_INTERIOR_MODULE`. The lift car, the landing leaves and ceiling and wall decoration carry no collision. Its readiness promise keeps the floor out of the draws while Physics prepares. A floor leaving the visible window calls `onDropBand`, including during admission; stale readiness cannot show it. Collision errors release the floor and report its identifier. One floor builds or prepares collision at a time.

`Elevators.mount(parcelId, floor, placement, modules, group)` binds the published `lift-car` and `lift-doors` placements to the shaft standing at them: the car is the module standing in the group that rides, and a landing's leaves are that module split at its own zero and hung on sliders, with the call plate beside them. Those copies never enter the shared draws. A core rect is read by its minimum corner.

A published room is its footprint, the fixtures the interior hung in it, and the surfaces its modules stand as, which is what its fill light is computed from. Rooms own no geometry: `RoomView` marks the ones in reach and on the player's level `visible`, nearest first.

`StreetLamps.build()` returns the complete `{group, posts, glows, dispose}` scene. `.stream()` returns nearby instances, source-derived whole-plan post reservations and admission ports in the street fixture schema. Source planning yields across frames and preserves source order: authored median poles, 19 m alternating posts, widest junction corners, 34 m plaza borders, 6 m separation, complete furnishing bases and overhead clearances. Uncovered walk legs receive wall-mounted fixtures on nearby facing facades. Spatial queries retain every overlapping authored volume.

Street fixtures belong to exactly one half-open cell by their final mounting position, independent of movement order. Cells are 128 m by default (16-256 m supported). Only nearby cells instantiate original pole, head, lens and wall-pack templates. New cells prepare rendering and requested collision before visibility. Later ports also prepare resident cells. Eviction cancels pending posts and releases instance buffers; return restores the same fixtures and glow identities. Disposal releases template geometry; factory materials stay shared. Invalid windows reject with `E_STREET_FIXTURE_WINDOW`; preparation and collision errors propagate after releasing the failed cell.
